R1-2610528 Feature lead summary #2 on NTN specific requirements and design Moderator (Qualcomm Incorporated)

Observation:

For 6GR NTN beam-specific k-offset, compared with cell-specific k-offset:

  • In scenarios where beams belonging to the same cell experience large differential delays, beam-specific k-offset reduces the scheduling latency (with respect to cell-specific k-offset) by aligning k-offset with the RTT of each beam. If not supported, cell-specific k-offset would need to be set according to the worst-case RTT across all beams of the cell

    • Note: For (quasi-)earth fixed cells, the beam or cell-specific k-offset might need to be adjusted accounting for the worst case RTT (of the cell or beam) for a period of time of the satellite pass to avoid frequent update of k-offset (except if time-varying k-offset is supported). In this case, scheduling latency benefits may not be realized.

  • Beam-specific k-offset may increase signaling overhead relative to a cell-specific value;

    • This overhead may be avoided or reduced depending on the system information design. For example, no added overhead will be present if the SIB carrying k-offset is beam-specific (if supported).

    • If SIB carrying k-offset is beam-specific (if supported), the UE will need to reacquire SIB upon moving to a new beam except if e.g. the network provides the beam specific k-offset for the new beam by some other means.

  • In RRC connected mode, the benefits of beam-specific k-offset may be realizable instead by UE-specific k-offset (if supported) except during contention based random access in connected mode. Using beam specific k-offset, however, may reduce overhead in case where UE specific k-offset is not needed in addition to the beam specific k-offset.

  • Note: If beam-specific k-offset is specified, further discuss whether/how to adapt it to the case of beams of different sizes (hierarchical beam structure)

Agreement:

For inter-satellite co-channel interference for same constellation/same-orbit scenario in 6GR NTN, RAN1 to evaluate potential gaps of the terrestrial CSI-IM/CSI-RS/SSB framework, taking into account:

  • Time-varying Doppler and timing offsets between serving and interfering satellites.

  • Measurement timing/alignment and validity under time-varying interference conditions.

  • Relevant scenarios e.g., co-channel interference from co-visible satellites with overlapping satellite coverage.

  • Whether CSI-IM/CSI-RS/SSB is needed

R1-2610530 Feature lead summary #4 on NTN specific requirements and design Moderator (Qualcomm Incorporated)

Observation:

For 6GR NTN time-varying broadcast (either cell-specific or beam-specific) K-offset, compared with fixed broadcast (either cell-specific or beam-specific) K-offset:

  • Time-varying K-offset can reduce the scheduling delay when the RTT changes during a satellite pass for earth-fixed cells. A fixed K-offset must cover the maximum RTT for that period.

  • The UE and the network must use the same K-offset at the same time, which may not be possible if the time-varying K-offset is provided by SIB update and different users re-acquire the SIB at different points in time.

    • If the UE and the network can deterministically and simultaneously calculate the K-offset without re-acquiring the SIB, this issue may be solved.

      • This may be based on e.g. satellite ephemeris, common TA (including drift rate and drift rate variation), a common time reference (e.g. SFN) for updating the K-offset, a reference point, and/or an update rule.

        • The update rule may include e.g. the method to update (from UE perspective) the K-offset under time-varying K-offset.

        • All these aspects would need to be investigated and other potential impact (specification impact, complexity, etc.) assessed.

  • In RRC connected mode, the benefits of time-varying k-offset may be realizable instead by UE-specific k-offset (if supported) except during contention based random access in connected mode. Using time-varying k-offset, however, may reduce overhead in case where UE specific k-offset is not needed in addition to the time-varying k-offset.

  • In RRC connected mode, except during contention based random access in connected mode, the benefits of time-varying k-offset may be realizable instead by adjusting the scheduling delays (e.g. K1/K2 or its equivalent in 6GR) if these scheduling delays allow for negative values.

  • A time-varying K-offset, for the duration it applies, would need to be larger than or equal to the worst-case UE RTT within the cell/beam for that duration.

  • A cell-specific K-offset in NR NTN is not a “fixed K-offset” since it can be updated via SIB-19, triggering the update is up to implementation. Some companies mentioned that current specifications do not guarantee that all UEs will acquire the SIB19 at the same time and, therefore, different UEs may apply different cell-specific K-offset values at the same point in time
