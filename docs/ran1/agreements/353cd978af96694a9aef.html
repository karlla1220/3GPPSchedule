Agreement:

At least the following parameters for satellite assistance info for UL time-frequency pre-compensation are supported for feeder link in 6GR NTN (at least for GNSS-based operation):

  • Common TA

  • Common TA drift rate

  • Common TA drift rate variation

  • For the feeder link in a transparent payload case UE assumes one of the following alternatives.

    • Alt 1: The feeder link Doppler is fully pre-compensated by the NW as in NR-NTN Rel-17.

    • Alt 2: The feeder link Doppler is pre-compensated by the NW in a way that, over the DL on service link, the observed time drift rate matches the observed frequency error.

    • Decide which alternative.

R1-2608593 Feature lead summary #2 on NTN specific requirements and design Moderator (Qualcomm Incorporated)

Agreement:

In 6GR NTN, it is supported that UEs with different GNSS operation modes can operate simultaneously in the same cell.

  • Network functionality is introduced to control access from UEs in different GNSS operation modes (e.g. to only allow UEs in a given GNSS operation mode to access the cell).

    • FFS: Which combination of GNSS operation mode(s) are allowed to be simultaneously enabled in the same cell.

    • FFS: Details

  • NOTE: The operation modes are:

    • GNSS-based

    • GNSS-degraded

    • GNSS-free/GNSS-less

Agreement:

For the 6GR NTN GNSS-less/free/degraded study, the solutions for UE pre-compensation recommended for specification in TR 38.742 are taken as starting point (except for PRACH physical channel which is discussed in the common agenda):

  • For open loop uplink time and frequency pre-compensation:

    1. For scenarios where UE cannot rely on its GNSS for UL timing and frequency pre-compensation on the service link, based on assistance information from the network.

    2. For cases where last known GNSS location is available and still sufficiently valid, this location is used for uplink open-loop time and frequency pre-compensation

      • FFS: How “sufficiently valid” is defined

  • Frequency adjustment command (in connected mode and RAR)

  • TA command enhancements at least in RAR

  • TA drift (e.g. based on FAC)

  • NOTE: Adjustments to the solutions in TR 38.742 can be proposed based on other decisions taken for 6GR.

R1-2608595 Feature lead summary #4 on NTN specific requirements and design Moderator (Qualcomm Incorporated)

Agreement:

For 6GR NTN, further study the following options regarding channel/signal assignment to wide/narrow beams for hierarchical beam structure.

  • Option 1:

    • Use wide beams (in DL) for SSB, common PDCCH, SIBs, paging

    • Use narrow beam for PRACH reception at aNB

      • Proponents to clarify how the UE selects the PRACH resource for different GNSS operating modes.

    • Use narrow beams for dedicated channels (DL + UL)

  • Option 2:

    • Use wide beams (in DL) for SSB

    • Use narrow beams (in DL) for common PDCCH, SIBs and paging

    • Use narrow beam for PRACH reception at aNB

      • Proponents to clarify how the UE selects the PRACH resource for different GNSS operating modes.

    • Use narrow beams for dedicated channels (DL + UL)

  • Option 3:

    • Use same beam (wide or narrow) for SSB, common PDCCH, SIBs, paging, PRACH reception at aNB during initial access.

    • Hierarchical beam structure is only applied for channels after PRACH reception

      • UE’s reporting of beam index can be based on PRACH (e.g. different ROs may correspond to indication of narrow beams), msg3 or subsequent messages. aNB selects the narrow beam for subsequent messages based on this reporting.

        • Proponents to clarify details on how the UE selects the beam index for the reporting for different GNSS operating modes.

      • Use narrow beams for dedicated channels (DL + UL)

  • Option 4:

    • Use same beam (wide or narrow) for all channels until completion of initial access.

    • Hierarchical beam structure is realized without enhancements with respect to NR techniques (for channels after initial access).

  • Note: For evaluation purposes, RAN1 assumes that the DL of wide beam and the DLs of the narrow beams within the wide beam operate on the same carrier using TDM and/or FDM.

Agreement:

For the 6GR NTN link budget template, for UEs in adverse propagation conditions (APC), “(28e) Other losses” = 9dB.

  • Other values, i.e. 6 dB and 15 dB maybe optionally used by companies when justified.

  • The following note from RAN1#126 also applies to this agreement.

    • NOTE: The study of a particular scenario for the link budget template does not imply that, if RAN1 determines a coverage gap for that scenario, it will be studied or specified how to close it.

Conclusion

The xls spreadsheet in R1-2608724 includes a summary of the LLS evaluations for 6GR NTN link budget (for information only). Further discussion in RAN1#127 to collect additional results with potential correction of existing results and discuss how to aggregate results.

The following two email discussions are correspondingly assigned.

  • Post-RAN1#126bis Email discussion 1:

    • October 20 – October 29

    • Topic: 6GR NTN remaining issues of evaluation assumptions (LLS and SLS)

  • Post-RAN1#126bis Email discussion 2:

    • November 9th – November 13th

    • Topic: 6GR NTN link budget LLS results collection

Observation:

On the issue of HARQ stalling in 6GR NTN, RAN1 observes the following

  • Solution 2:

    • Some companies observe that having a PDSCH / PUSCH span multiple slots will result in reduced throughput and increased coverage (relative to PDSCH/PUSCH spanning single slot) if these multiple slots are used for different RVs / repetitions of the same TB without increasing the TB size.

    • Some companies observe that, if the multiple slots are used to carry different coded bits of the same TB without repetitions while increasing the TB size proportionally to the number of slots, the throughput is not reduced (relative to PDSCH/PUSCH spanning single slot). In this case, the soft buffer requirement at the UE might need to be increased (similar to increasing the number of HARQ processes).

      • Some companies mention that multi-slot transmission increases the TB size and retransmission granularity, which may degrade throughput.

    • For transmission of different coded bits of the same TB across multiple slots, the throughput and UE soft-buffer impact depend on the supported TBS, number of transmission layers.

      • Some companies indicated that a UE may support TN operation and larger TBS sizes and/or number of transmission layers than the ones applicable to NTN, and therefore the transmission of different coded bits of the same TB across multiple slots for NTN operation may not increase the total UE soft-buffer size in all cases.

    • The observations above regarding UE soft-buffer impact may also apply to the circular buffer used for transmission (at both UE and aNB).

    • Some companies mentioned that issues related to soft-buffer and circular buffer used for transmission may be at least partially resolved by LBRM (if specified).

    • Some companies indicate that Solution 2 does not reduce the NTN HARQ RTT or directly allow earlier reuse of a HARQ process. Its benefit for HARQ stalling would instead come from reducing the rate at which new HARQ processes are consumed.

    • Some companies mention that Solution 2 is a generic coverage enhancement solution (if different RVs/repetition are used) or scheduling solution (if different coded bits of the same TB across multiple slots are used) which does not directly address the HARQ stalling problem and may affect TN/NTN harmonization if defined only for NTN.

    • Some companies mentioned that Solution 2 might increase aNB / UE complexity due to the need of performing buffering of multiple slots before decoding. On the other hand, some companies mention that this aNB/UE complexity depends on the specification details of Solution 2 (if specified).

    • Other potential impact of Solution 2 (e.g. L2-buffer, rate matching, UCI multiplexing) may be studied in a different agenda item (if justified in that agenda item).

    • If NTN agenda item decides to specify Solution 2 as an NTN-specific feature (which may affect TN/NTN harmonization), further discussion may occur in this agenda item in the future.

  • Solution 4:

    • Increasing the number of HARQ processes to accommodate the NTN RTT (at least for some orbits) was introduced in Rel-17 with the specification of 32 HARQ processes.

    • Increasing the number of HARQ processes increases the number of transport blocks that can be scheduled within one RTT and therefore resolves HARQ stalling in cases where a smaller number of HARQ processes cannot cover the whole RTT (for FR1-NTN and FR2-NTN).

    • Extending solution 4 to fully cover cases where the RTT is very large (e.g. MEO or GEO scenarios) may not be feasible. In this case, however, an increased number of HARQ processes may partially alleviate HARQ stalling.

    • Some companies observe that solution 4 may have impact on UE complexity due to increased soft buffer size requirement. On the other hand, some companies mention that increasing the number of HARQ processes does not necessarily require a proportional increase in the total UE soft-buffer size. The soft-buffer impact depends on the maximum supported TBS, number of transmission layers, and whether a fixed total soft buffer can be shared among the configured HARQ processes.

    • The number of HARQ processes required to accommodate the whole HARQ RTT depends on at least the RTT, slot duration, scheduling rate, and traffic assumptions.

    • The number of HARQ processes may affect for example the HARQ-process-number signaling and the corresponding DCI design (e.g. required number of bits).

Agreement:

For 6GR NTN, further discuss the target number or range of HARQ processes
