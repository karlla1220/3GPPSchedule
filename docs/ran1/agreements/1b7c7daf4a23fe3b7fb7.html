For Objective 2 in the WI:

  • The following techniques that are transparent to RAN1 specifications have been discussed in RAN1:

  • Technique A (Same cell): Broadcasting a position and/or velocity vector which is different from the actual one at the satellite (using existing signaling in SIB19), which may be combined with an appropriate common TA to minimize time difference (up to an offset equal to an integer number of slots) and/or frequency difference with respect to UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation.

  • Technique B (Same cell): Transmitting genuine ephemeris and receiving with two different time-frequency hypotheses at the SAN, or by TDM’ing and/or FDM’ing the different groups of UEs across different sets of resources and using a single time-frequency hypotheses for each set of resources.

  • Technique C (Two cells in the same carrier): the network can provide two cells in the same carrier (by either TDM or FDM), one for “UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation”, and one for “UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation (including potential modifications introduced in the Rel-20 GNSS resilience work item)”

      1. Indication of the CRP (compensation reference point, expressed as lat/long, FFS: altitude) to the UE in broadcast system information

      2. The UE can assume that the timing drift is zero at the CRP.

      3. The UE can assume that the Doppler is zero at the CRP.

      4. The UE uplink timing pre-compensation is modified (with respect to the current one as defined by uplinkPrecompensation-r17) by subtracting a term equal to the RTT between the CRP and the SAN

      5. The UE uplink frequency pre-compensation is modified (with respect to the current one as defined by uplinkPrecompensation-r17) by adding a term equal to the one-way uplink Doppler between the CRP and the SAN, when the nominal UL carrier frequency is considered as the reference frequency for UL compensation.

    • For Technique 1b (which is an implementation of Technique 1 in WID based on explicit indication of Doppler and timing parameters):

      3. The UE can assume that the Doppler is zero at the CRP.

      4. Introduce negative common TA, whose absolute value corresponds to RTT between satellite and CRP.

      5. The UE uplink frequency pre-compensation is modified (with respect to the current one as defined by uplinkPrecompensation-r17) by adding a term determined by the common Doppler information.

      • For the same carrier case, corresponding to the same-cell analysis above, the network configures independent cells for the two types of UEs in the same carrier. In the downlink, the gNB needs to transmit one independent set of SSB/SIB1 broadcast signals for each cell, and therefore the downlink resource overhead increases noticeably compared with the single-cell scenario. Although the common-channel overhead doubles, the proportion of common-channel overhead itself is relatively low, and thus even after doubling, the resulting overhead remains acceptable. Taking the RO (PRACH format 0) as an example, one RO occupies only 1 ms within the 20 ms in the time domain, and occupies only 6 PRBs within the 106 PRBs of a 20 MHz bandwidth in the frequency domain.

      • Meanwhile, the gNB needs to maintain two independent downlink time-frequency references and handle the relative drift between them caused by satellite movement, which can be handled by gNB implementation. In the uplink, the gNB needs to indicate the pre-compensation parameters to the NTN UE and handle the corresponding uplink arrival timing within each cell. Although the time-frequency references of the two cells are independent of each other, the gNB can still align the slot boundaries within each cell by configuring common TA and TAC, and handle the frequency difference between the two cells separately, which is feasible and acceptable for the gNB. Since the UE behavior within each cell remains consistent with Rel-17, and the time-frequency difference between the two cells is entirely handled by the gNB at the scheduling and receiver sides, it is still feasible for the gNB to handle two independent cell configurations.

These optional mechanisms do not remove the need for independent access control and modifications to RAN2 specs.

  • [R1-2610028] (ETRI) Evaluated the implementation-based techniques quantitatively (LEO 600 km, 2 GHz, quasi-earth-fixed cell with the CRP at the cell center):

    • Actual ephemeris: The common TA and TA commands can align the UL timing of UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation with that of UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation up to an integer number of slots, with the common TA updated every few seconds. Their UL frequency offset, e.g., about 80 kHz (30°) and 46 kHz (60°) with a UE frequency reference from the DL, cannot be removed by Rel-17/18/19 signaling.

    • Synthetic ephemeris, Alt 1: The UL frequency error of these UEs can be smaller than that of UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation only under conditions on the cell size, elevation angle, timing error budget, validity duration, UE frequency reference and UE propagation algorithm (not specified). With a UE frequency reference from the DL and a propagation algorithm unknown to the network, the error is larger for small cells (e.g., r = 5 km at 30° to 60°). With the Rel-17 common TA and a timing error budget of Te_NTN, no operating point exists with a 10 s validity duration (e.g., r = 25 km at 30°). The matching common TA has a negative quadratic term, which ta-CommonDriftVariant cannot represent.

    • Synthetic ephemeris, Alt 2 (stationary satellite near the CRP): There is no gain in UL frequency. With ta-Common = 0, the timing error becomes asymmetric, e.g., −311 µs to −22 µs for r = 25 km at 30°. With two-body propagation, the frequency error near the CRP grows to about 0.66 kHz after 5 s and 7.9 kHz after 60 s.

    • Rel-20 enhancement: With UL pre-compensation with respect to the CRP, the residual errors are at the level of the UE position and ephemeris errors, e.g., about 0.3 Hz and 0.06 µs for a UE position error of 10 m (r = 25 km, 30°), compared to several hundred Hz with a synthetic ephemeris. Rel-20 UEs implementing the enhancement can operate in the same cell as UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation without the above conditions and costs.

  • [R1-2608412] (Qualcomm) Operation in the same cell is not possible based on current specification due to the following:

    • DL compensation: Rel-17+ NR NTN UEs do not expect the downlink to be pre-compensated. The performance (e.g. in terms of tracking and demodulation performance) of Rel-17+ NR NTN UEs in a cell with downlink pre-compensation is unpredictable.

    • UL compensation: Rel-17+ NR NTN UEs perform uplink pre-compensation assuming that the downlink is not pre-compensated and the uplink is not post-compensated. Transmission time and frequency of a Rel-17 NR NTN UE will be completely misaligned with the network receive window (in time and frequency).

    • Timing relationships: Rel-17+ UEs expect K-offset to be larger than the RTT. UEs not capable of performing pre-compensation will not apply K-offset. This will lead to incompatible timelines (at least during initial access)

    • Barring: RAN2 specifications state that the barring bit in MIB should be set to barred in an NTN cell.

    • These issues can be solved by specifying enhancements under Technique 1 in Rel-20.

  • [R1-2609849] (Apple)

    • Operating UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation (including potential modifications introduced in the Rel-20 GNSS resilience work item) and UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation in the same cell is difficult, spectrally inefficient, and leads to performance degradation.

    • Under existing specifications, the barring mechanism prevents concurrent operation. If coexistence is forced, the network must handle dual timing and frequency hypotheses for PRACH and manage slot alignment via separate common TA/TAC. In the downlink, pre-compensation introduces a tracking mismatch for Rel-17/18/19 UEs that use ephemeris and GNSS location for tracking, causing severe reception degradation unless they rely solely on downlink signals (SSB/TRS).

    • To solve these issues, specifying Rel-20 enhancements under Technique 1 (residual delay and Doppler compensation relative to the CRP) is required to ensure ideal time-frequency synchronization and optimal uplink reception.


  • Justifications for same carrier:

    • [R1-2610028] (ETRI) Two cells on the same carrier are possible, but at a cost compared to a single cell (15 kHz SCS, one SS/PBCH block beam per cell):

      • FDM is not possible for a 5 MHz carrier. The loss of DL resources is about 3% (20 MHz) to 7% (10 MHz).

      • TDM: The loss is about 2% (20 MHz) to 7% (5 MHz) if the DL frame timing of the two cells does not drift relative to each other. Under Technique 1, it drifts by up to about 20 µs/s at the satellite, and the switching guards increase the loss to about 5% to 26%.

      • A static split of resources reduces the maximum carried load when the traffic of the two types of UEs is unbalanced, e.g., to about 71% for a 70/30 traffic split with a 50/50 resource split.

      • For a 20 MHz carrier, the overhead of the duplicated common channels alone is small (about 2% to 3%). The main costs are elsewhere, i.e., narrow carriers, the switching guards and the static split.

  • [R1-2608412] (Qualcomm) Operating separate cells for NR NTN and “UEs not supporting uplink pre-compensation” would require doubling the overhead of common channels. According to the assumptions under the Rel-19 NTN DL enhancements, for Set 1-1, the overhead of operating two cells will increase to 80%. Operating two cells will require the network to maintain two timing and frequency assumptions. Additionally, there are no mechanisms to make a Rel-17 NR NTN UE prioritize the NTN cell in this scenario.

  • [R1-2609849] (Apple)

    • Operating UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation (including potential modifications introduced in the Rel-20 GNSS resilience work item) and UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation in two separate cells (via TDM or FDM) on the same carrier is inefficient in terms of spectrum utilization and network implementation.

    • Transmitting separate broadcast common channels and configuring separate RACH occasions for two cells doubles the common channel signaling overhead compared to single-cell operation.

    • Because the two cells exhibit different uplink timing and frequency behaviors, maintaining orthogonality between the TDM/FDM cells requires two timing/frequency hypotheses at the network receiver as well as additional guard periods or guard PRBs, which reduces spectrum efficiency and increases scheduling complexity.

  • [R1-2607885] (Ericsson) One carrier can host multiple cells if and only if the channel bandwidth is wide enough (e.g., ≥ 10 MHz CBW), otherwise, if the CBW is small (e.g., 5MHz CBW or 3 MHz CBW) in principle only one cell per carrier is expected
