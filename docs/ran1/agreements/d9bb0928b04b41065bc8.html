  • For the companies that think Rel-20 enhancements are needed, on the potential impact and/or potential modifications to NR-NTN and related procedures/signaling, the following specification impact on UE time-frequency assumptions and UE pre-compensation is observed:

    • For Technique 1a (which is an implementation of Technique 1 in WID based on CRP indication):

      1. Indication of the CRP (compensation reference point, expressed as lat/long, FFS: altitude) to the UE in broadcast system information

      2. The UE can assume that the timing drift is zero at the CRP.

      3. The UE can assume that the Doppler is zero at the CRP.

      4. The UE uplink timing pre-compensation is modified (with respect to the current one as defined by uplinkPrecompensation-r17) by subtracting a term equal to the RTT between the CRP and the [SAN]

      5. The UE uplink frequency pre-compensation is modified (with respect to the current one as defined by uplinkPrecompensation-r17) by adding a term equal to the one-way uplink Doppler between the CRP and the SAN, when the nominal UL carrier frequency is considered as the reference frequency for UL compensation.

    • For Technique 1b (which is an implementation of Technique 1 in WID based on explicit indication of Doppler and timing parameters):

      1. Indication of the common TA/Doppler information between CRP and SAN to the UE in broadcast system information

      2. The UE can assume that the timing drift is zero at the CRP.

      3. The UE can assume that the Doppler is zero at the CRP.

      4. Introduce negative common TA, whose absolute value corresponds to RTT between satellite and CRP.

      5. The UE uplink frequency pre-compensation is modified (with respect to the current one as defined by uplinkPrecompensation-r17) by adding a term determined by the common Doppler information.

The UE uplink frequency pre-compensation is modified (with respect to the current one as defined by uplinkPrecompensation-r17) by adding a term equal to the one-way uplink Doppler between the CRP and the SAN, when the nominal UL carrier frequency is considered as the reference frequency for UL compensation.

      2. The UE can assume that the timing drift is zero at the CRP.

      4. Introduce negative common TA, whose absolute value corresponds to RTT between satellite and CRP.

      5. The UE uplink frequency pre-compensation is modified (with respect to the current one as defined by uplinkPrecompensation-r17) by adding a term determined by the common Doppler information.

    • For Technique 2, the specification impact is {a, c, e} above (based on either 1a or 1b).

    • Other techniques have been proposed assuming different combinations of (a-e) above, including:

      • Technique 3: Network perform UL post-compensation of service link Doppler, the specification impact is {a, e} (based on either 1a or 1b).

      • Technique 4: Network perform UL post-compensation of service link Doppler and service link delay, the specification impact is {a, e, d} (based on either 1a or 1b).

      • Technique 5: Network perform UL post-compensation of service link delay only, the specification impact is {a, d} (based on either 1a or 1b).

      • Technique 6: Network perform DL pre-compensation of service link Doppler and service link delay, the specification impact is {a,b,c} (based on either 1a or 1b)

  • Technique 1a/1b sets the Doppler and timing drift to be zero (up to error tolerances) at the CRP, and can align (at the SAN) the uplink transmissions of all NTN UEs applying the technique to that of a UE not performing uplink pre-compensation located at the CRP.

  • From RAN1 specification it is allowed that different UEs are served in uplink in the same cell even if their uplink transmissions are not aligned at the SAN and

    • Some companies observe that this alignment of uplink transmission of all NTN UEs is not necessary to support simultaneous operation with “UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation”, and aspects such as inter-user interference can be handled by network scheduling so that performance can be maintained.

    • Some other companies observe that a lack of uplink alignment will lead to reduced performance, reduced system efficiency, inter-user interference and increased network complexity.

  • Some companies mentioned concerns on the feasibility of keeping the Doppler and timing drift to be zero (up to error tolerances) at the CRP continuously. Some other companies mentioned that current satellite systems can achieve Doppler and timing drift to be zero (up to error tolerances).

  • Some companies observe that zero Doppler and timing drift (up to error tolerances) at the CRP is not necessary to support the simultaneous operation with “UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation”. Some other companies observe that in that case the performance of “UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation” cannot be maintained.

  • Some companies observe that this alignment of uplink transmission between “UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation (including potential modifications introduced in the Rel-20 GNSS resilience work item)” and “UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation” can also be achieved by the existing NR NTN RAN1 specification with no additional overhead according to the observation in RAN1#126bis and it is not necessary to modify the existing specification to support simultaneous operation with “UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation”. Some other companies disagree (based on the arguments in the same agreement in RAN1#126bis).

  • The performance benefit of Technique 1a/b over existing NR NTN specification operating in a system performing uplink/downlink compensation was not evaluated quantitatively (e.g. by system level simulations).

    • Some companies mentioned that a qualitative evaluation is sufficient.

    • Some companies mentioned this lack of quantitatively results is due to lack of time in the study phase.

    • Some companies mentioned this lack of quantitatively results is due to the fact that a Rel-17 NR NTN operating in a downlink compensated cell is operating in non-nominal conditions and, therefore, its behavior cannot be modeled according to current 3GPP specifications. Some companies think this modeling is possible.

  • With respect to time-frequency error, some companies observe that Technique 1a/1b offers similar performance (in terms of relative timing-frequency error) to that of a Rel-17 NR NTN UE in a network without pre-compensation. Some other companies observe that the interference described in the next bullet will impact time-frequency error.

  • For Technique 1a/b, for multi-beam operation, the following is observed:

    • The application of different time-frequency pre-compensation to different cells/beams served by the same satellite will increase the SAN complexity (with respect to a SAN serving Rel-17 NR NTN only)

      • Some companies mentioned that current SAN implementations serving ”UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation” are already performing downlink and uplink pre/post-compensation and therefore there is no complexity increase for these deployments.

    • The application of different time-frequency pre-compensation to different cells/beams served by the same satellite will result in different beams in the same frequency losing orthogonality.

      • This orthogonality lost will result in interference across adjacent beams at least in some cases (if e.g. adjacent PRBs in the same slot and/or the same PRB in consecutive slots scheduled in adjacent beams). Some companies observe that this interference will result in reduced system efficiency, while other companies observe that this issue can be handled by appropriate scheduling and network implementation. Some companies observe that if Technique 1a/1b is applied in a cell-specific manner, there will be no interference across beams of the same cell. Some companies observe that Technique 1a/1b reduces the loss of orthogonality (and resulting interference) across satellites.

  • Some companies have observed that, under Technique 2 (and under any technique in which only one of b/c is specified), the relation between timing drift and Doppler is not maintained, which will impact performance of UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation.

  • Some companies observed that, under Technique 2, since UL timing pre-compensation is not modified, the issues identified in RAN1#126b for coexistence with UEs not performing UL pre-compensation (e.g. 2 timing hypotheses for PRACH) remain.

  • One company has observed that the specification of d) may impact the capability of the network to determine the UE location based on uplink transmission (since all UEs in a cell arrive at the gNB with the same timing). This company proposes that UE TA reporting (as defined in Clause 5.4.8 and 6.1.3.56 of TS 38.321), could be modified to report the residual timing compensation in reference to the CRP can be used to determine the UE location.


    • For any of the techniques above, regarding coexistence of “UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation (including potential modifications introduced in the Rel-20 GNSS resilience work item)” in the same cell as UEs supporting the techniques above, the following techniques have been proposed:
