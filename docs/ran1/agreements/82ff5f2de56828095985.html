R1-2607735 FL Summary #5 on DL Transmission Scheme for DL Shared Channel Moderator (Ericsson), Moderator (Google)

Observation:

  • For study of frequency interleaving of PDSCH:

    • The following options are considered for span of the frequency interleaving:

      • Option 1: Within all scheduled/allocated RBs

      • Option 2: Within all RBs of the configured/predefined interleaving bandwidth(s)

      • Option 3: Within all scheduled/allocated RBs within each of the configured/predefined interleaving bandwidth(s)

    • Three cases have been identified for the case of contiguous PDSCH frequency-domain allocation for a single UE (before interleaving) and the following is observed for regarding relationship between the above options for the different cases:

      • Case A: For the case when the configured/predefined interleaving bandwidth coincides with all indicated scheduled/allocated RB(s)

        • Options 1/2/3 are the same

      • Case B: For the case when the total aggregated interleaving bandwidth across all configured/predefined interleaving bandwidth(s) coincides with all indicated scheduled/allocated RB(s)

        • Option 1 and Option 2/3 are different

        • Option 2/3 are the same

      • Case C: For the case when the total aggregated interleaving bandwidth across all configured/predefined interleaving bandwidth(s) is larger than all indicated scheduled/allocated RB(s)

        • Option 1/2/3 are different

    • On performance of frequency interleaving:

      • 7 sources provided preliminary simulation results for frequency interleaving for contiguous PDSCH frequency-domain allocation (before interleaving):

        • Simulation results applicable to Case A and Options 1/2/3:

          • 1 source (Apple) reports 0—8 dB gain compared to no interleaving

          • 1 source (Huawei) reports 0—1.8% gain compared to no interleaving

          • 1 source (Lenovo) reports that the performance gain of interleaving degrades as delay spread increases

          • 1 source (Qualcomm) reports ~1.5 dB and ~0.7 dB at SNR of 25 dB for 400 MHz and 100 MHz channel bandwidth compared to no interleaving

          • 1 source (Tejas) reports 1—3 dB gain compared to NR VRB-to-PRB mapping

          • 1 source (vivo) reports 4.88%, 4.93%, and 6.23% gain at SNR values of 15 dB, 25 dB, and 35 dB compared to NR VRB-to-PRB mapping

          • 1 source (ZTE) reports 15.8% gain compared with 5G localized VRB-to-PRB resource mapping and 7.8% gain compared with 5G distributed VRB-to-PRB resource mapping

        • Simulation results applicable to Case B and Options 2/3:

          • 1 source (Huawei) reports 0.4% performance difference in case of using 100 MHz interleaving bandwidth for the 200 MHz PDSCH allocation compared 50, 100 MHz interleaving bandwidth; and 0.1% performance difference in case of using 200 MHz interleaving bandwidth compared to 100 MHz

          • 1 source (Lenovo) reports that performance gain of interleaving across 400 MHz compared to interleaving within 200 MHz decreases as delay spread increases

        • Simulation results applicable to Case C:

          • No simulation results applicable to Case C

        • Note: Simulation assumptions may not be aligned across companies and further alignment is encouraged

    • On complexity of frequency interleaving:

      • A larger bandwidth of interleaving may provide improved frequency diversity but may increase interleaver complexity

      • Bandwidth of interleaving and granularity of interleaving, and relationship between bandwidth and granularity should be carefully studied considering impact on performance and complexity

    • Additional aspects that were discussed for frequency interleaving:

      • NR VRB-to-PRB mapping is one realization of Option 2 with a single configured/predefined interleaving bandwidth coinciding with the BWP.

      • For fallback DCI RBG-based resource allocation, which typically has larger overhead compared to RIV-based resource allocation, is not supported in NR.

      • 1 source mentions that NR VRB-to-PRB mapping may cause RB collision between UEs with and without interleaving and between interleaving UEs with partially overlapping bandwidth, which has been identified as a pain point of NR VRB-to-PRB mapping. Some companies do not share the same view.

      • 1 source mentions that Option 2 may cause similar issues as NR VRB-to-PRB mapping for the case when the total aggregated interleaving bandwidth across all configured/predefined interleaving bandwidth(s) is larger than all indicated scheduled/allocated RB(s), i.e., for Case C. Some companies do not share the same view.

      • 1 source mentions that Option 2 may provide performance gain over Option 1 and 3 in the case of a small number of scheduled/allocated RB(s). Some companies do not share the same view.

      • 1 source mentions that to address the concern of performance degradation of Option 1 compared to Option 2 for RIV-based frequency-domain resource allocation, RBG-based resource allocation may be used to achieve similar performance. When RBG size is large, RBG-based resource allocation may not be spectrally efficient for transmitting a small packet. Some companies do not share the same view.

Agreement:

  • For single PDSCH with frequency domain resource allocation between 200 MHz and 400 MHz, down-select from the following options (down-select to one option at RAN1#127):

    • Option A: TB can span up to 400MHz

    • Option B: TB can span up to 200 MHz (FDM of TBs)

      • Two TBs in the frequency domain.

      • Option B-1: Single MCS across TBs

      • Option B-2: Separate MCS per TB

      • FFS: Same/separate HARQ retransmission of TBs

      • FFS: Other aspects

    • For decision, consider additional aspects including UE and NW complexity, power consumption, performance.
