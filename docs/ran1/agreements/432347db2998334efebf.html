Observation:

For 6GR PUSCH DMRS sequence design with CP-OFDM, the following starting point, candidate approaches and classifications by design objective are observed.

Common starting point

Gold sequence based DMRS initialized by a scrambling ID, taking the NR design as a starting point, e.g., [ZTE/Sanechips, R1-2609197; Spreadtrum/UNISOC, R1-2609175; Xiaomi, R1-2609368; vivo, R1-2609520; CATT, R1-2609676; Samsung, R1-2609606; Apple, R1-2609860; NTT DOCOMO, R1-2610418; CMCC, R1-2609416; NEC, R1-2609452; Nokia R1-2609083; LGE R1-2609634; Ofinno R1-2609822; Qualcomm R1-2610473; Jio R1-2610560 P8.].

Category 1. CDM-group-specific sequence differentiation for PAPR reduction

  • Option 1a: Apply CDM-group-specific sequence differentiation/scrambling-ID-based mechanism for PAPR reduction, e.g., [Xiaomi, R1-2609368; CATT, R1-2609676; Apple, R1-2609860; CMCC, R1-2609416; NEC, R1-2609452; Samsung, R1-2609606; Nokia, R1-2609083; LGE, R1-2609634; Ofinno, R1-2609822; Qualcomm, R1-2610473].

    • FFS: If any other comb than comb-2 supported, ways to reduce PAPR characteristics of other combs (>2) need to be studied. E.g., [Nokia, R1-2609083]

  • Option 1b: Generate the DMRS root sequence on an RE-by-RE basis across the PUSCH bandwidth to provide sequence differentiation among CDM groups, e.g., [ZTE/Sanechips, R1-2609197, vivo, R1-2609520].

  • Option 1c: CDM group-specific phase sequences multiplied onto the basic sequence of different CDM groups based on a specific granularity, e.g., [vivo, R1-2609520].

  • Option 1d: The DMRS ports intended to be orthogonal within a CDM group use the same DMRS sequence, e.g., [Apple, R1-2609860].

Category 2. Sequence enhancement for interference mitigation, including short-sequence cases

  • Option 2a: Optimize Gold-sequence initialization or sequence selection for short DMRS sequences, including consideration of the time dependence of initialization and optimized initializer sets, e.g., [Ericsson, R1-2610268].

  • Option 2b: Apply supplementary sequences on top of the Gold sequence to improve correlation and interference properties, including supplementary phase and amplitude components targeting inter-cell and inter-port interference, e.g., [Huawei/HiSilicon, R1-2609314].

  • Option 2c: Use dedicated computer-generated or numerically optimized short-sequence sets, including CGS-based low-interference sequences, e.g., [vivo, R1-2609520], and optimized constant-modulus sequences, e.g., [Ericsson, R1-2610268].

  • Option 2d: Introduce a time/frequency-varying factor across DMRS symbols/REs of TD/FD-OCC length per DMRS port in PUSCH DMRS sequence generation, e.g., [ZTE/Sanechips, R1-2609197].

Category 3. Sequence configuration and selection flexibility for MU-MIMO and mTRP

  • Option 3a: Support more than two DMRS scrambling IDs for mTRP and multi-beam scenarios without increasing DCI overhead, e.g., [Nokia, R1-2609083].

  • Option 3b: Support at most two configured DMRS scrambling IDs, e.g., [Jio, R1-2610560]

  • Option 3c: Associate DMRS sequence initialization with the transmission configuration, such as the SRS resource set, TCI state or TRP, together with the corresponding configuration and indication mechanisms, e.g., [Xiaomi, R1-2609368; Google, R1-2609991; ZTE/Sanechips, R1-2609197].

  • Option 3d: Dynamically select the scrambling ID per scheduling instance from a configured candidate set, including two [Apple, R1-2609860] or four RRC-configured candidates, e.g., [ZTE/Sanechips, R1-2609197].

Category 4. Modulation of Gold-based DMRS

  • Option 4a: QPSK-modulated Gold sequence, e.g., [Apple, R1-2609860; LGE, R1-2609634; Nokia, R1-2609083; NTT DOCOMO, R1-2610418; Samsung, R1-2609606; Jio, R1-2610560]

  • Option 4b: 8PSK-modulated Gold sequence, e.g., [ZTE/Sanechips, R1-2609197]

Category 5. Others

  • Option 5a: Commonality of the DMRS sequence between regular DMRS and sparse DMRS, e.g., [KDDI, R1-2610659].

  • Option 5b: Common CP-OFDM DMRS sequence design between PDSCH and PUSCH, e.g., [Apple, R1-2609860].

Observation:

For 6GR PUSCH DMRS sequence design with DFTS-OFDM, the following candidate approaches and their classifications are observed.

Common starting point:

ZC sequence and CGS in 5G NR as a starting point, e.g., [Spreadtrum/UNISOC, R1-2609175; Lenovo, R1-2609551; LGE, R1-2609634; Apple, R1-2609860; NTT Docomo, R1-2610418; CMCC, R1-2609416; NEC, R1-2609452; CATT, R1-2609676]

Category 1. Enhancement/optimization of ZC sequence construction

  • Option 1a: Polynomial-phase sequence construction, e.g., [Huawei/HiSilicon, R1-2609314].

Category 2. ZC root selection/extension and sequence-pool configuration

  • Option 2a: Enhanced frequency-domain ZC root selection targeting low PAPR/CM, including root-index candidate selection and allocation-size-dependent root-selection approaches, e.g., [Xiaomi, R1-2609368; Samsung, R1-2609606; Nokia, R1-2609083].

  • Option 2b: Time-domain ZC sequence generation followed by transformation to the frequency domain, with optimization of root selection, e.g., [NTT DOCOMO, R1-2610418; Nokia, R1-2609083].

  • Option 2c: Enlargement/configuration of the ZC base-sequence pool, including the number of sequence groups and sequences per group, considering the allocation bandwidth, e.g., [Nokia, R1-2609083].

  • Option 2d: ZC sequence root selection considering both slope-matching principle and low PAPR, e.g., [vivo, R1-2610796]

Category 3. Enhanced computer-generated sequences

  • Option 3a: Enhanced CGS obtained through numerical/AI-assisted search, targeting PAPR, sequence-pool size and correlation properties, while retaining frequency-domain constant modulus for the considered non-π/2-BPSK cases, e.g., [vivo, R1-2609520; Xiaomi, R1-2609368].

  • Option 3b: Enhanced short sequences for π/2-BPSK-related DMRS, including re-selection within the existing sequence space [ZTE/Sanechips, R1-2609197], searches with time-domain constant-/non-constant-modulus property [Huawei/HiSilicon, R1-2609314], and AI-assisted sequence search [vivo, R1-2609520].

  • Option 3c: Resource-specific block-based sequence construction using stored sequence blocks indexed by absolute frequency resources, optionally with per-block phase offsets, targeting independence from allocation start and length, e.g., [Ericsson, R1-2610268].

  • Option 3d: Study enhanced short CGS using higher-order PSK alphabets, including 8PSK, 16PSK, 32PSK and 64PSK, to reduce PAPR, e.g., [Xiaomi, R1-2609368].

Category 4. PN-based sequence design

  • Option 4a: Frequency-domain QPSK-modulated PN sequences for selected higher-order PUSCH modulations. The proposed applicability differs: 16/64/256/1024-QAM in ZTE/Sanechips’ contribution, and 64/256/1024-QAM in Samsung’s contribution, e.g., [ZTE/Sanechips, R1-2609197; Samsung, R1-2609606].

Category 5. Golay-based sequence construction

  • Option 5a: Golay-based sequences for the long-sequence case associated with π/2-BPSK, targeting frequency-domain flatness and auto-correlation properties, e.g., [Huawei/HiSilicon, R1-2609314].

Category 6. Others

  • Option 6a: Independence from allocation start and length for flexible MU-MIMO pairing, e.g., [Ericsson, R1-2610268].

  • Option 6b: Possible common sequence design between SRS and PUSCH DMRS for DFTS-OFDM with QPSK and higher-order PUSCH modulations, e.g., [Nokia, R1-2609083; Ericsson, R1-2610268].

  • Option 6c: Refinement of NR Type-2 low-PAPR sequences or introduction of a new low-PAPR sequence design, including consideration of whether one sequence type could suffice, e.g., [TCL, R1-2610789].
