Observation

The following observations on UPT performance vs. CSI feedback overhead have been made for the SD basis selection options of the 6GR CSI codebook design for the high-resolution scheme (W1W2Wf), until RAN1#126bis:

For Opt1 (L=1 only, layer-specific/group-specific SD basis selection):

  • 11 sources showed benefits in UPT vs CSI feedback overhead trade-off compared to Opt3 (layer-common DFT basis selection) for various carrier frequency (4/7 GHz), number of ports (up to 256 ports), traffic load/model, UE speed, and SU/MU-MIMO, at least in lower PMI overhead regime

    • 5 sources showed that L=1 achieves higher UPT than Opt3 (including Rel-16 eType-II), but with up to ~40 to 75% overhead reduction [Samsung, ZTE, Ericsson, Nokia, CATT]

    • 1 source showed 7% UPT gain over Opt3 [InterDigital]

    • 1 source showed <=3% UPT gain over Opt3 of the overhead regime from 100~250 bits [MediaTek]

    • 2 sources showed 1.5% UPT gain over Opt3 with L=1[Lenovo, xiaomi]

    • 1 source showed up to 1.31% UPT gain over Opt3 [OPPO]

  • 3 sources showed Opt1 does not exhibit such UPT benefit over Opt3 [vivo, Huawei, Qualcomm]

For Opt2 with L>1 layer-specific/group-specific SD basis selection (Note that Opt2 also includes L=1, i.e., Opt1):

  • 15 sources (including the 11 sources for Opt1) showed benefits in UPT vs CSI feedback overhead trade-off compared to Opt3

    • 7 sources showed that L=2 or 3 achieves higher UPT than Opt3 (layer-common DFT basis selection), with up to ~40 to 66% overhead reduction, [Samsung, ZTE, Ericsson, CATT, Xiaomi, Lenovo, CEWiT]

    • 1 source showed that L=2 or 3 achieves up to ~5 to 10% UPT gain over Opt3 at comparable overhead, or alternatively achieve similar UPT with ~ 20-50% overhead reduction [Qualcomm]

    • 2 sources showed that L=2 achieves up to 21% UPT gain over Opt3 with the same L [InterDigital, Intel]

    • 3 sources showed a higher UPT gain over Opt3, i.e., 3.12%~6.05% UPT gain for L≥2 [OPPO], ≤2% for L≥2 [MediaTek, Huawei]

  • 5 sources showed that the UPT gain saturates at L=3/4, and large L (e.g., 4, 6) brings little additional gain while increasing overhead [Samsung, CATT, Lenovo, Ericsson, ZTE]

For Opt3 (L>1 layer-common SD basis selection):

  • 4 sources showed comparable or better UPT over Opt2 in higher-overhead regime (>200 bits)

    • 2 sources showed that Opt3 with Rel-16 eType-II with PC6 provides the highest UPT gain over Opt2 [Qualcomm, Intel]

    • 1 source, assuming PMI calculation based on a reduced-dimension EVD, showed that Opt3 tends to outperform (within 4% UPT) than Opt2, and comparable only with full-dimension per-SB EVD [vivo]

    • 1 source showed that Opt-3 can outperform over Opt-2 with at most 3% UPT gain, and the gain becomes less when the number of L increases. [MediaTek]

  • 1 source showed that Opt3 with enhanced PCs (L=3/4, pv=1/8, β=1/2) outperforms Opt2 (with beta=1) at similar overhead [Huawei]

  • 1 source showed Opt 3 (layer-common combined DFT basis selection) has 4% cell edge UPT gain over Opt1 at the similar overhead [Apple]

  • 2 sources showed that the UPT of Opt3 can be enhanced by replacing DFT SD basis with non-DFT SD basis (synthesized from linear combination of DFT beams) up to 4.1% over the Opt3 with Rel-16 eType-II PC6 [Xiaomi] and up to 2% for cell edge UPT over the Opt3 at comparable PMI overhead [Apple]

    • 1 source showed Opt3 (synthesized from linear combination of DFT beams) with L=2 has up to 2.1% UPT gain over Opt1 [Xiaomi]

    • 1 source showed Opt3 (synthesized from linear combination of DFT beams) has up to 1.2% UPT gain over Opt2 for L=2 [Xiaomi]

    • 1 source showed Opt3 (synthesized from linear combination of DFT beams) with L=2 or 3 achieves higher UPT than Opt3 with layer-common DFT basis, with up to ~40 to 66% overhead reduction. [Xiaomi]

  • 1 source showed that Opt3 with L=2 provides only marginal performance degradation compared to Opt2 with L=2 while reducing the overhead by 15% [LGE]

  • 2 sources showed that Opt3 with additional overhead optimization schemes can achieve performance comparable UPT to Opt2, e.g., delay-domain pre-alignment without bitmap achieves comparable to layer-specific L=3 at similar overhead of 200~350 bits [Qualcomm], or modified PCs (L=2/3/4/6/8, pv=1/8, 1/4, beta=1/2, 3/8, 5/16) [OPPO]

  • 5 sources showed that Opt3 is worse than Opt1/Opt2 with up to 5% UPT loss in the higher PMI overhead regime [Samsung, ZTE, Ericsson, Lenovo, CATT]

In terms of number of FLOPs (floating point operations) needed for PMI calculation, comparing layer-specific SD basis selection vs. layer-common SD basis selection, the results seem to be varied due to differences in UE implementation assumptions:

  • 3 sources showed ~1~1.5x FLOPs, since WB covariance calculation is dominant for both schemes [Samsung, ZTE, Ericsson]

  • 1 source showed ~1.2~1.6x FLOPs for L=1 and 1.7~2.2x FLOPs for L=2 due to per-layer projection for inter-layer interference cancellation [Xiaomi]

  • 1 source showed ~1.5~2.5x FLOPs for L=1 and ~2.5x~3.5x FLOPs for L=4 due to per-layer projection for inter-layer interference cancellation [MediaTek]

  • 1 source showed ~2~3.5x FLOPs [OPPO]

  • 1 source showed 2.5~6.5x FLOPs even when low-complexity inter-layer interference cancellation is assumed, depending on channel bandwidth and number of Tx ports. [Qualcomm]

  • 1 resource showed that the complexity of CSI calculation for high-res codebook can be even lower than that of for low-res codebook depending on the UE implementation [ZTE]

  • Note: the complexity ratio above is computed with respect to the FLOPs for the layer-common SD basis selection.

Note: 32~256 ports, 2.1/3.5/4/7 GHz, SU/MU-MIMO and FTP traffic are assumed; RI up to 4 (up to 8 in 1 source).

Note: different UE implementation algorithms for layer-specific SD basis selection (e.g., inter-layer interference cancellation, full- or reduced-dimension EVD) are assumed by sources, which impact both performance and complexity.

The following observations on UPT performance vs. CSI feedback overhead have been made for the 6GR low-resolution scheme (W1W2), at least for RI=1-4, until RAN1#126bis:

For layer-specific vs. layer-group-specific spatial-domain (SD) basis selection:

  • 9 sources showed that layer-group/layer-pair-specific SD basis selection (or reduced SD candidates) with overhead and/or complexity reduction achieves smaller than or comparable UPT to the layer-specific SD basis selection

    • 3 sources showed that layer-group/layer-pair-specific SD basis selection offers ~30% overhead reduction and/or PMI calculation complexity reduction at the expense of 1~3% UPT loss [ZTE, MediaTek, Qualcomm]

    • 1 source showed the layer-group-specific basis selection provides ~2%(mean)/~4%(5%=tile) UPT gain at high UE speed (60 km/h) [Qualcomm]

    • 2 sources showed higher UPT loss over layer-specific SD basis selection, e.g., ~5.5% [Samsung] or ~6.1% (sub-band) / ~8.7% (wideband) [CATT].

    • 2 sources showed 2% UPT loss over layer-specific SD selection with overhead reduction of up to 21 bits [OPPO, Xiaomi]

    • 1 source showed layer-group-specific SD basis selection based on basis constructed from combined 2D DFT beams can achieve comparable performance with layer-specific SD basis selection for wideband CSI with ~10 bits report overhead increase and subband CSI with ~20 bits report overhead saving [Xiaomi]

    • 1 source showed the layer-group specific SD basis selection (≤2 beams for rank 2-4) on par with layer-specific with large complexity reduction [Ericsson]

    • 1 source showed that layer-pair-specific SD basis selection achieves UPT gain over layer-specific at lower overhead with synthesized beams [Apple]

    • 1 source showed that layer-group SD basis selection (with combined DFT basis) can achieve 2% of mean UPT gain than layer-specific SD basis selection at lower CSI feedback overhead for WB CSI feedback [Apple]

    • 1 source showed that layer-group basis selection (with combined DFT basis) can acheive5% of mean UPT gain than layer-specific SD basis selection at comparable CSI feedback overhead for SB CSI feedback [Apple]

For layer-specific vs. layer-common (q1,q2):

  • 2 sources showed UPT gain of layer-specific (q1,q2), e.g., ~7% (sub-band) / 8.7% (wideband) over layer-common (q1,q2) [Samsung, Ericsson]

For inter-polarization co-phase alphabet:

  • 5 sources showed that BPSK achieves mean UPT comparable to QPSK with overhead and/or complexity reduction, especially for sub-band co-phase

    • <0.1% mean UPT loss with 37~42% overhead reduction (sub-band, layer-specific/group-specific) [Samsung]; ~2% mean UPT loss with ~30% overhead reduction [MediaTek]; ~2% mean UPT loss for wideband co-phase [Qualcomm]; no UPT loss [OPPO, Ericsson]

  • 2 sources showed UPT gain of QPSK over BPSK, especially for wideband co-phase

    • BPSK shows 5.1~7.5% (mean) / 6.6~9.9% (5%-tile) UPT loss for wideband with comparable overhead, and 2.5~4.1% / 4.1~7.2% loss for SB with 36~42% overhead reduction [CATT]; 1.5% mean UPT loss for WB with only ~3 bits overhead saving [Samsung]

For SD basis set:

  • 3 sources showed no or marginal gain of orthogonal basis selection in 1D direction over 2D direction, e.g., ~0% or 1.36% mean UPT gain with 25% more overhead [Samsung, Qualcomm, CATT]

  • 1 source showed UPT gain with extended orthogonal beam set (orthogonal in at least one dimension) [Nokia]

  • 2 sources showed UPT gain with basis constructed from combined 2D DFT beams [Xiaomi, Apple]

For UE complexity,

  • 5 sources showed that the complexity of layer-specific SD basis/co-phase selection can be reduced by BPSK, layer-group-specific SD or reduced search space, e.g., up to 96% total complexity reduction [ZTE], reduction of the number of W2 hypotheses from 256 to 4 [Qualcomm], 83.6%~96.1% searching space reduction [OPPO], 1~17% CMAC reduction [MediaTek], up to 5x complexity reduction [Xiaomi], and a marginal UPT loss with reduced search space [InterDigital].

  • 1 source showed that the complexity of layer-specific SD basis/co-phase selection has 25x (larger complexity) compared to layer group specific SD basis selection [Ericsson]

  • 1 source showed that the complexity of layer-specific SD basis/co-phase selection with layer-specific (q1,q2) with sequential processing of layers has 0.5x (smaller  complexity) than layer-group specific SD selection [Ericsson]

  • 1 source showed that the complexity of layer-specific SD basis/co-phase selection with BPSK has 3x (larger complexity) compared to layer group specific SD basis selection [Ericsson]

Note: 32~256 ports, 2/3.5/4/7 GHz, SU/MU-MIMO and FTP traffic are assumed.

The following observations on UPT vs. CSI feedback overhead trade-off have been made for the comparison between the low-resolution scheme (W1W2) and the high-resolution scheme (W1W2Wf) of the 6GR CSI codebook, until RAN1#126bis:

  • It has been identified that there are scenarios where high-resolution offers superior UPT vs. CSI overhead trade-off than low-resolution. Yet, in other scenarios, the low-resolution provides superior UPT vs CSI overhead trade-off. Such scenarios can be differentiated based on (but not limited to) reporting granularity (WB/SB), MIMO scheduling assumption (SU-/MU-MIMO), UE speed, the number of CSI-RS (antennas) ports, frequency band, and the use of uncalibrated passive antenna arrays. Therefore, the low-resolution and high-resolution schemes are evidently complementary to ensure maximum performance in terms of UPT vs. CSI overhead trade-off over these various scenarios.

    • WB vs. SB reporting:

      • WB CSI reporting: 4 sources showed that in WB scenario, the low-resolution scheme with smaller overhead provides UPT comparable to the high-resolution scheme [Ericsson, Samsung, Nokia, Apple]

      • SB CSI reporting: 2 sources showed that in SB scenario, the high-resolution scheme achieves ~8% higher UPT than the low-resolution scheme with comparable or larger overhead [Samsung, Nokia]. It is evident that SB precoding is much more beneficial for the high resolution scheme especially for higher frequency selectivity (e.g., 128 ports at 7 GHz) than WB precoding [Nokia]

    • SU-MIMO vs. MU-MIMO:

      • SU-MIMO: 5 sources showed that the low-resolution scheme can provide UPT comparable to the high-resolution scheme in SU-MIMO with smaller overhead, e.g., up to ~3~8% UPT gap in SU-MIMO [Ericsson, Samsung, Nokia, Apple, MediaTek]

      • MU-MIMO: 3 sources showed that the UPT gain of the high-resolution scheme over the low-resolution scheme becomes larger in MU-MIMO, e.g., up to 11% [MediaTek], with a larger margin, e.g., up to 15% in MU-MIMO than in SU-MIMO [Ericsson, Apple]

    • Low/moderate vs. high UE speed:

      • low/moderate UE speed: 4 sources showed that the high-resolution scheme offers comparable or better UPT at low/medium UE speed [Ericsson. Samsung, ZTE, Nokia]

      • high UE speed: 1 source showed that the low-resolution scheme becomes more robust (in terms of UPT) than the high-resolution scheme at higher UE speed (e.g., ~60 km/h) [Qualcomm]

    • Smaller vs. larger number of antenna ports:

      • Larger number of ports: 3 sources showed that the UPT gain of the high-resolution scheme over the low-resolution scheme increases with the number of ports, e.g., from ~4% (64 ports, 4 GHz) to ~6% (128 ports, 7 GHz) [Nokia], and the high-resolution scheme with a larger L becomes beneficial for 256 ports while saturating for 32 ports [CEWiT, OPPO]. 1 source showed that the UPT gain of the high-resolution scheme over the low-resolution scheme is almost same for the 64-ports, 4GHz and 128-ports, 7GHz [Qualcomm].

      • Smaller number of ports: 2 sources showed that the UPT gain of the high- over the low-resolution scheme is smaller for smaller number of ports [Nokia, Ericsson]

    • Uncalibrated passive antenna arrays:

      • 2 sources showed that enhancements to the low-resolution scheme to support 4Tx (uncalibrated passive) antenna arrays can provide up to 14-15% average UPT gain and up to 32-44% cell edge UPT gain [Nokia, Ericsson].

    • Higher rank (RI>4) transmission:

      • 2 sources showed that in such scenarios, e.g. 128 ports RI=8, the low-resolution scheme requires lower overhead (~28 bits) than the high-resolution schemes (~200 bits or more) [Nokia, Qualcomm]

    • 9 sources showed that the UPT gain of the high-resolution scheme over the low-resolution scheme increases with CSI feedback overhead (linear trend of UPT w.r.t. overhead) [Samsung, Ericsson, Nokia, Huawei, Xiaomi, ZTE, OPPO, Qualcomm, vivo].

    • 1 source showed that the UPT gain increases further as the traffic load increases (RU from 30% to 70%) [Nokia], while 1 source showed similar UPT gain as the traffic load increases (RU from 30% to 70%) [Qualcomm],

  • 7 sources provided the complexity analysis for the low-resolution and the high-resolution schemes.

    • 3 sources showed that the complexity of the high-resolution scheme is comparable to or lower than that of the low-resolution scheme [Samsung, ZTE, Ericsson]

    • 4 sources showed that the complexity of the high-resolution scheme is higher than that of the low-resolution scheme. [MediaTek, Qualcomm, OPPO, Xiaomi]

  • 1 source observed that the low-resolution scheme can be suitable for low-tier/IoT devices (e.g., WB-only PMI, max RI=1) [Sony].

  • Potentially common design between the low-resolution and the high-resolution schemes at least from specification perspective (this may or may not imply common UE implementation):

    • 9 sources observed that the low-resolution W1W2 and high-resolution W1W2Wf can potentially use a common W1 design (e.g., L=1 layer-specific SD basis) [Samsung, Sony, Ericsson, ZTE, Nokia, FHR, Ofinno, InterDigital, ETRI]. And 1 source observed that the low-resolution W1W2 and high-resolution W1W2Wf can potentially use a common W1 design (e.g., layer-group-specific synthesized beam SD basis) [Apple].

    • For some design, e.g., layer-common SD basis selection or L>1 layer-(group)specific SD basis selection, the common design between the low-resolution and the high-resolution schemes may or may not be possible.

  • Note: 4~256 ports, 2/3.5/4/7 GHz, SU/MU-MIMO and FTP traffic are assumed.

  • Note: the observation can be further updated based on new results in future meetings.

  • Note: above observations are based on DFT basis. Other basis will be discussed separately.
