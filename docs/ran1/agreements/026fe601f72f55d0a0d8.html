Observation

Beam operation during 6GR initial access has been studied by companies, including beam acquisition during initial access, beam report/refinement during initial access.

  • Source [ETRI] observes that early beam reporting in idle mode may enable beam-aware DL transmissions, e.g., of paging and system information, and thereby reduce unnecessary transmissions.

  • Source [CATT, CICTCI] observes that a second RS, e.g., CSI-RS or an on-demand SSB, can be used for beam refinement during initial access, with the refined DL beam indicated implicitly by the PRACH resource, so that it applies from Msg1, or explicitly in Msg3, so that it applies from Msg4, and that early CSI and beam reports can be carried as MAC CEs.

  • Source [Huawei, HiSilicon] observes that SSB-based beam measurement during initial access provides limited beamforming gain and insufficient UL support, that with a predefined narrow-beam-to-PRG mapping the SIB1 DMRS can be reused for DL narrow-beam measurement without additional RS overhead; On-demand signal can also be applied for DL narrow-beam refinement, together with multi-port/multi-beam PRACH repetition, can further refine the UE Tx precoder and the BS Rx beam within the RA procedure, with overhead scaling with the number of ongoing RA attempts; that a fine beam determined from channel statistical information achieves an average gain of 9.6 dB over the coarse SSB sweeping baseline.

  • Source [MediaTek, Xiaomi] observes that coarse spatial information obtained from a wide-beam SSB can be refined before the random access procedure with on-demand/additional reference signals, improving time/frequency synchronization, UL coverage and random access capacity.

  • Source [OPPO] observes that beam refinement or beam prediction can improve the link performance of Msg2/Msg4/Msg6, but increases the Msg1/Msg3 payload, which degrades the UL transmission that is the bottleneck during initial access, and increases the RS overhead and BS energy consumption if it relies on CSI-RS.

  • Sources [Tejas, 1Finity] consider that additional beam operation during initial access should be introduced only where an end-to-end comparison shows a net gain in access success or latency, or with an initial access latency comparable to NR and minimum signalling overhead, respectively.

  • Source [vivo] observes that beam refinement may improve the transmission performance of Msg2/Msg4/Msg6, but increases the MsgA/Msg3 payload, which degrades the UL transmission performance that is the bottleneck during initial access. Besides, RS configurations would increase the UE baseband complexity if beam refinement is performed on CSI-RS.

  • Source [ZTE, Sanechips] observes that early beam reporting can achieve high-performance link from the beginning of RRC CONNECTED state based on additional RS (e.g., CSI-RS) and rapidly selection of candidate coordinated TRPs and reduce measurement overhead of early CSI acquisition operation during initial access.

  • Source [Ericsson] observed that although UE-side wide-to-narrow beam prediction is feasible, allocating RACH resources for UE-predicted narrow beams increases NW overhead and the system-level gain remains unverified. Although UE-side wide-to-narrow beam prediction is feasible, reporting early beam predictions (e.g. in Msg3) and indicating the corresponding capability (e.g. in Msg1) increases message sizes, and the system-level gain remains unverified.
