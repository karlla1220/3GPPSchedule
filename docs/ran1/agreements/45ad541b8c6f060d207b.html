Observation

For multi-bit SR, the following study/evaluation results are observed:

  • About the performance comparison of multi-bit SR and 1-bit SR +BSR:

    • One source (R1-2610811, Nokia) reported that at the same aggregated UL resource overhead or at the same latency, multi-bit SR with more than 2 bits has a higher latency or higher overhead than 1-bit SR + BSR with a 10 ms SR period, unless every configured UE requests UL resources at least every 22 to 138 ms (equal overhead) or 22.5 to 144 ms (equal latency), depending on PUCCH format.

    • One source (R1-2609623, Samsung) reported that with the same UL resource overhead, the latency gain of multi-bit SR over 1-bit SR + BSR is limited to small packets at low load, depends on the PUCCH resource and the false alarm rate of multi-bit SR, and can be lost with a coarse quantization at high load.

    • One source (R1-2609689, CATT) reported that counting only the SR PUCCH and the BSR PUSCH, multi-bit SR on 2-symbol PUCCH format 2 has a mean latency of 3.75, 6.25 and 18.75 ms at mean inter-arrival times of 20, 100 and 600 ms, against 10 ms for 1-bit SR + BSR. With the over-grant counted and the upper edge of the level granted, multi-bit SR fits within the same overhead only from 5 bits against a Short BSR, and then has a latency of 3.75 to 5 ms against 10 ms at all evaluated arrival rates.

    • One source (R1-2610482, Qualcomm) reported that assuming same periodicity for 1-bit SR and multi-bit SR, the latency is reduced by one round trip, however, this comes at higher cost of SR resources depending on the PUCCH format (up to 18x compared to 1-bit SR), and assuming same resource overhead for 1-bit SR and multi-bit SR, the multi-bit SR has higher latency due to its longer periodicity

    • One source (R1-2609146, MediaTek) reported that multi bit SR provides ~1–3.4 ms average latency reduction, with relative gains up to ~35% under PDCCH sparse or low SR period configurations. As expected, the gain magnitude scales with PDCCH monitoring density and SR activation periodicity.

    • One source (R1-2609294, ZTE) reported that multi-bit SR can achieve the low latency with the same resource overhead compared with 1-bit SR when the number of UE is 20. In case 100UEs, the 2 bits SR still have the better performance. Compared with 1-bit SR, 4 bits SR has a lower average latency but a higher latency for 95% due to more retransmissions caused by low reliability. In case 600 UEs, 2 bits SR still have better performance in terms of the average latency and 95% UE latency.

    • One source (R1-2610794, Huawei/HiSilicon) reported that under a light to medium load (UE number = 10~40, traffic arrival rate = 0.01pkt/s, and packet size = 50KB), 2-bit SR shows {40%, 25%, 7.14%} reduc-tion for {50%-tile, 90%-tile, and 95%-tile} latency over the 1-bit SR baseline with 10 UEs, and {25%, 18.75%, 10.53%} reduction for {50%-tile, 90%-tile, and 95%-tile} with 40 UEs.

    • One source (R1-2609425, CMCC) reported that under the same SR configuration, multi-bit SR reduces the mean UL scheduling latency compared with 1-bit SR + BSR, by removing one BSR round trip. The tail gain is further enlarged by the BSR PUSCH HARQ retransmissions in the benchmark, and under the same aggregated UL resource overhead, the latency gain of multi-bit SR widens with system load, as the saved probe-grant budget allows denser SR occasions.

    • One source (R1-2609563, Tejas) reported that, when the first grant covers the buffer, a 2-bit SR with an upper-edge first grant lowers the mean latency by 3.6–4.9 ms compared with 1-bit SR + BSR (6.7–7.5 ms instead of 11.1–11.7 ms at a 2.5 ms SR period). Counting non-data overhead only, the 2-bit SR needs more overhead than 1-bit SR + BSR for the same latency at small budgets, mainly because of the 4× PUCCH multiplexing cost and the data-sized grants issued on false alarms (about 66% of its non-data overhead at a 1% false-alarm rate and 100 ms inter-arrival), and it is up to 4.9 ms faster above about 0.18–0.35 RB-symbols per ms per UE (0.24–0.41 with a cyclic-shift spacing of 2). Counting the PUSCH scheduled for the BSR, it is 0.9–19.9 ms faster at every common overhead for 20 and 100 ms mean inter-arrival times. Compared with a 1-bit SR answered by a configured large first grant, the 2-bit SR gives the same latency with 31–59% less non-data overhead (7–39% with a cyclic-shift spacing of 2).

  • About the performance comparison of multi-bit SR and BSR in CB PUSCH:

    • One source (R1-2609069, InterDigital) reported that under high offered load multi-bit SR can provide lower resolution information for initial UL grant scheduling at equivalent resource overhead compared to CB-BSR and under moderate offered load CB-BSR can support significantly higher numbers of UEs with comparable control resource overhead.

    • One source (R1-2609154, Ericsson) reported that in the scenarios studied, M-bit (M>2) SR requires substantially higher uplink resource overhead (up to 100x, depending on the scenario) than CB-BSR in order to be achieve comparable phy layer latency of CB-BSR. And CB-BSR provides a more efficient utilization of uplink resources than M-bit (M>2) SR while maintaining comparable PHY-layer latency

    • One resource (R1-2610794, Huawei/HiSilicon) reported that in the TDD scenario, with the same aggregated UL resource overhead, multi-bit SR shows {86.67%, 90.91%} reduction for {90%-tile, 95%-tile} than CB-BSR with 20 UEs and {86.67%, 90.91%} reduction for {90%-tile, 95%-tile} with 100 UEs.

  • About the bit number carried in multi-bit SR:

    • One source (R1-2609689, CATT) reported that with exponential levels and the grant at the upper edge of the indicated level, the mean over-grant is 44%, 9.2% and 1.1% at 3, 5 and 8 bits, about ten times lower than with uniform levels, and at least 5 bits are needed to match the Short BSR of NR

  • About the link coverage of PUCCH carrying multi-bit SR:

    • One source (R1-2610811, Nokia) reported that multi-bit SR with more than 2 bits loses about 2 dB or more of coverage relative to 1-bit SR.

    • One source (R1-2610794, Huawei/HiSilicon) reported that compared with the legacy 1-bit SR, 2-bit SR provides additional information while incurring virtually no cost using sequence-based PUCCH format (e.g. NR PUCCH format 1).

    • One source (R1-2609425, CMCC) reported that the link coverage of a 2-bit multi-bit SR on a sequence-based PUCCH structure is within about 0.5–0.8 dB of the 1-bit SR baseline at a 1% error target, corresponding to a cell-range reduction of about 3–5%. The coverage impact of a 4-bit multi-bit SR remains within about 1 dB.

    • One source (R1-2609623, Samsung) reported that ccompared with 1-bit SR, multi-bit SR needs 0.8 to 1.2 dB more SINR for 2 to 3 bits with PF0 cyclic shift selection, 1.3 to 3.3 dB more for 3 to 8 bits with PF2, 1.2 dB more for 2 bits with PF1, and 1.5 to 3.4 dB more for 3 to 11 bits with PF3 (4 Rx, 1% false alarm, 1% error rate).

    • One source (R1-2609689, CATT) reported that at a false alarm probability of 1%, a 3- to 5-bit SR needs 1.5 to 2.3 dB more Es/N0 than a 1-bit SR on two PUCCH symbols, and 0.2 to 0.8 dB more on 14 symbols, and a 1-bit SR on 14-symbol PUCCH format 1 needs 7.3 to 8.1 dB lower Es/N0 than a 3- to 5-bit SR on 2-symbol PUCCH format 2, and one PRB carries 84 such SRs.

    • One source (R1-2609563, Tejas) reported that, at 1% false alarm and 1% SR error (CDL-A 100 ns, 4 Rx), a 2-bit SR needs 0.8–0.9 dB more SNR than a 1-bit SR on PF0 and PF1 when the code-point is carried by cyclic-shift or OCC selection or by four 1-bit SR configurations, 1.3 dB more with an explicit QPSK payload on PF1, and a 3-bit SR with cyclic-shift selection on PF0 1.2 dB more. With cyclic-shift or OCC selection, almost all errors are missed SRs (wrong code-point rate below 1.2 × 10−4), whereas the QPSK payload gives about 3 × 10−3 wrong code-points, i.e., wrongly sized grants.

Agreement

For SR in 6GR, 1-bit SR for 6GR is supported.

At most one of 2-bit SR and CB-PUSCH based BSR will be selected
