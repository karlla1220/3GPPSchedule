Agreement

For study of multiple carriers where for initial access a UE can utilize resources for UL (Msg1, etc) or for both UL (Msg1, etc.) and DL (Msg2, etc.) in one carrier other than the carrier where the UE obtains the system information providing configuration for those resources, consider the following potential scenarios for the set of candidate physical carriers for which resources are provided for initial access:

  • S1: 1 DL physical carrier + N UL physical carriers, where N > 1

  • S2: M DL physical carriers + 1 UL physical carrier, where M > 1

  • S3: M DL physical carriers + N UL physical carriers, where M > 1 and N > 1

Note 1: The carrier where the UE obtains the system information and other candidate physical carrier(s) are counted above.

Note 2: For a given UE and a given initial access procedure, UE may use only a subset of the candidate physical carrier.

Note 3: The inclusion of a scenario for study does not imply agreement to support it. The need and applicable use cases/deployment scenarios for each scenario are subject to further study.

Note 4: For S2 and S3, M DL physical carriers may include carrier(s) only providing reference signals for initial-access UL transmissions on candidate UL carriers.

Conclusion

For UC-1 (UL coverage enhancement / DL–UL coverage imbalance), compared with single-carrier initial access on a mid-/high-frequency carrier, idle/inactive (if supported) mode multi-carrier operation which enables Msg1/Msg3 transmission on a lower-frequency UL carrier while retaining DL reception on the mid-/high-frequency carrier can provide benefit in terms of coverage of UL transmission of initial access for UEs with sufficient DL coverage but insufficient UL coverage on the mid-/high-frequency carrier.

For UC-2 (RACH capacity / offloading / load balancing), compared with single-carrier RACH capacity enhancements, idle mode multi-carrier operation which makes additional UL access resources available across candidate carriers can provide additional benefit in terms of RACH capacity and access-load distribution, particularly where the SI-providing carrier remains a bottleneck despite applicable single-carrier enhancements.

  • Where DL random-access responses become a bottleneck, additional candidate DL carriers can provide further offloading opportunities beyond those achieved through UL resource distribution alone.

Agreement

For further study of multiple carriers where for initial access a UE can utilize resources for UL (Msg1, etc) or for both UL (Msg1, etc.) and DL (Msg2, etc.) in one carrier other than the carrier where the UE obtains the system information providing configuration for those resources, study the following combinations of scenarios and deployment conditions, with the associated study aspects:

  • S1 with D1

    • Study the following aspects:

      • UL access carrier selection

      • Reference signal-to-RO mapping

      • Spatial relation determination for UL transmissions

      • UL Tx power control (e.g., pathloss calculation/estimation)

    • FFS whether or not S1 with D1 is applicable to S1 with D2/D3

  • S3

    • Consider the following sub-scenarios:

      • S3a: One UL carrier from N UL carriers is selected for UL (Msg1, etc.) initial access transmissions. Only the SI-providing DL carrier is used for initial-access DL transmissions (e.g., Msg2/Msg4), while DL reference signals on other DL carriers can be used by the UE, e.g. for sync and/or pathloss estimation purposes for initial-access UL transmissions

      • S3b: One UL carrier from N UL carriers is selected for UL (Msg1, etc.) initial access transmissions. The DL carrier associated with the selected UL carrier is used for initial-access DL transmissions (e.g., Msg2/Msg4)

    • Study the following aspects:

      • UL access carrier selection

      • Reference signal-to-RO mapping

      • Synchronization acquisition including RS assumptions on multiple DL carriers

      • UL transmission timing determination and TA acquisition

      • Spatial relation determination for UL transmissions

      • UL Tx power control (e.g., pathloss calculation/estimation)

      • UE measurement behaviours

      • NW/UE energy efficiency

      • Association between DL carrier(s) and UL carrier(s)

      • Detailed procedure compared to single-carrier based cell reselection

      • Applicability to D1/D2/D3

  • Candidate carrier information and the associated initial access resource configuration is provided via SI on the SI-providing carrier.

Note 1: FFS whether S2 can be accommodated as a special case of S3 with a single candidate UL carrier, without requiring separate design.

Note 2: Other combinations of scenarios and deployment conditions are not precluded from further study, subject to justification of their applicability and additional benefits.

Note 3: The inclusion of a combination for study does not imply agreement to support it.
