For 6GR SRS sequence design, the following candidate options and their classifications are observed.

Category 1: Enhancement/optimization of ZC sequence

  • Option 1: Enhanced ZC sequence design based on sampling a piecewise LFM chirp at a rate higher than the sweeping bandwidth, e.g., [QUALCOMM, R1-2608425]

  • Option 3: Sequence construction via concatenation of segments of ZC sequence, with each rotated by a per-segment phase offset, e.g., [CATT, R1-2607411]

  • Option 8: Polynomial phase sequence, e.g., x(n)=e−j2π(cn3+an2+bn)/N , e.g., [HW, R1-2607472]

  • Option 9: Phase optimized ZC sequence, i.e., based on Gerchberg-Saxton algorithm, e.g., [Google, R1-2607986] , based on optimized cyclic shift, e.g., [PML, R1-2607013]

Category 2: ZC sequence root selection/extension

  • Option 6: Enhancements to ZC sequence root selection, e.g., time-domain root selection for ZC-based sequence, e.g., [Nokia, R1-2607069], [QUALCOMM, R1-2608425], [DoCoMo, R1-2608354], [LGE, R1-2607965], configurability of v value by aNB , e.g., [Apple, R1-2608234], new ZC root determination equation for frequency domain ZC sequence mapping, e.g., [Samsung, R1-2607575], [Xiaomi, R1-2607215], [DoCoMo, R1-2608425], ZC sequence root selection considering both slope-matching principle and low PAPR, e.g., [vivo, R1-2607350] , an affine extension of time-domain ZC root selection, e.g., [ETRI, R1-2608643]

  • Option 7: ZCbased sequences as in NR, and expand the sequencegroup number (e.g., from 30 to 46, 58 or 70) and the basesequence number (e.g., from 2 to 3, 4, 5 or 6) to enlarge the SRS rootsequence pool, e.g., [ZTE, R1-2607009], [PML, R1-2607013]

Category 3: Time/frequency-domain sequence design

  • Option 2: Time-domain/Frequency-domain design e.g., Costas-based sequence design, e.g., [MTK, R1-2608613]

Category 4: Enhanced CGS

  • Option 4: Sequence construction via concatenation of sequence blocks, with each rotated by a per-block phase offset, e.g., [Ericsson, R1-2607758]

  • Option 5: Sequence construction via concatenation of sequence blocks, without separate phase rotation per block, e.g., [Ericsson, R1-2607758]

  • Option 10: Enhanced CGS with expanded phase-rotation alphabets (e.g., 64-PSK or higher) and/or with new lengths, e.g., [vivo, R1-2607350] , [Ericsson, R1-2607758], [ETRI, R1-2608643], [LGE, R1-2607965], [Nokia, R1-2607069], [ZTE, R1-2607009]

  • Note: For short sequences, Option 10 may serve as a complement to the other longsequence approaches, rather than an alternative for downselection.

Category 5: ZC and CGS sequences in NR

  • Option 11: ZC and CGS sequences in NR with lengths ≤ X

    • Study how to support larger SRS sequence lengths

  • Cyclic-shift (CS) based port is supported without TDM multiplexing for 2-, 3-, 4- and 8-port SRS

  • Comb-based port multiplexing is supported without TDM multiplexing for 3-, 4- and 8-port SRS

  • FFS the necessity of TDM multiplexing of cyclic-shift (CS) based port and comb-based port for supporting different number of SRS ports

Agreement

Study at least the following aspects of TD-OCC:

  • Candidate covering codes: e.g., Hadamard code, and nonHadamard codes such as DFT code.

  • How to design TD-OCC to support all repetition factors.

  • The performance under partial dropping, and the feasibility of retaining orthogonality-preserved SRS ports under partial dropping, instead of dropping all ports. That is, only the SRS ports that lose orthogonality due to the dropped symbol(s) are discarded, while the remaining SRS ports that can still maintain orthogonality using the surviving symbols are retained for CSI acquisition.

  • TD-OCC hopping for interference randomization

Agreement

Two-level frequency hopping of 6GR SRS, study the following hopping patterns for each level:

  • First level: hopping over BW with one or multiple frequency hops

    • Option 1: SRS subband nested tree-like frequency hopping pattern as in NR

    • Option 2: Hopping pattern defined by RB offsets which are derived based on NW indication, details of NW indication is FFS

    • Option 3: Sequential hopping patterns

  • Second level: hopping within each frequency hop of the first-level hopping

    • Option 1: SRS subband nested tree-like frequency hopping pattern as in NR

    • Option 2: Partial SRS subband sounding with/without intra-repetition hopping (with PF=2 and 4 as baseline)

◦            FFS: PF beyond the NR Rel-17 values, e.g., 8 and 16

  •  Option 3: Subband or partial SRS subband Sequential hopping patterns

Note: this does not mean that two-level hopping is supported and single-level hopping is precluded.

Agreement

For evaluation of 6GR SRS sequence designs, adopt the following assumption on XC calculations for sequences of the same length

  • XC is measured in time domain over all delay lags

  • For M number of sequences of the same length, frequency-domain overlapping assumption 1(full overlapping) is used

  • The magnitude of normalized periodic cross-correlation between distinct sequences ri and rj at shift ls  (where ls∈{0,1,…,N−1}) is computed as

Ri,j(ls)=1EiEj|∑n=0N−1ri(n)⋅rj*((n−ls)modN)|

where Ei=∑n=0N−1|ri|2

  • Paring of M sequences of the same length, M(M-1)/2 pairs in total are taken into account

  • For each pair of sequences, the cross-correlation metric is defined as the maximum value of the normalized delay cross-correlation spectrum

  • For M sequences of the same length, collect the mean values of the normalized delay cross-correlation spectrum for all pairs and derive the following statistics: maximum, average, CDF@10%, 50%, 90%.
