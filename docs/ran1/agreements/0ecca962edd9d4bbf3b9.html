Observations:

The following observations are based on evaluation methodology agreed in RAN1-126bis

  • Observation A: (Qm,R) preference dependency on rank

    • A-1: 9 sources (Samsung, Ericsson, ZTE/Sanechips, Huawei/HiSilicon, CATT, OPPO, Xiaomi, ETRI/SJTU, DCM) observe that higher-order modulation with lower coding rate is preferred under evaluated high-rank/full-rank, closed-loop fading-MIMO conditions, particularly rank 4 with MMSE reception. Benefits generally become larger or occur at more SE points than in low-rank cases.

  • Observation B: (Qm,R) preference dependency on frequency selectivity

    • B-1: 6 sources (Apple, CATT, OPPO, Fraunhofer IIS/HHI, LG Electronics, CMCC) provide analysis or fading-channel examples supporting higher-order modulation with lower coding rate at selected SE points when sufficient fading/frequency diversity is exploitable. CMCC observes this at lower SE.

    • B-2: 1 source (LG Electronics) observes that enabling intra-slot frequency hopping reverses the preferred modulation/code-rate combination in its evaluated narrowband PUSCH cases.

  • Observation C: (Qm,R) preference for open loop channel:

    • C-1: 6 sources (Samsung, CATT, OPPO, Huawei/HiSilicon, Apple, Qualcomm) report that lower-order modulation with higher coding rate can provide gains at fixed SE under evaluated open-loop configurations, especially when rML receiver is used.

  • Observation D: (Qm,R) preference for high spatial layer imbalance channels assuming NR codeword-to-layer mapping

    • D-1: 9 sources (Qualcomm, Samsung, MediaTek, Jio Platforms, Apple, Nokia, OPPO, ETRI/SJTU, Ericsson) provide results or analysis supporting higher-order modulation with lower coding rate under strong spatial-layer imbalance (such as CDL-A channel with rank 4) within a codeword. Benefits depend on the evaluated conditions and SE points;

  • Observation E: (Qm,R) preference for well balanced channel (AWGN like, spatially balanced)

    • E-1: 5 sources (Apple, CATT, CMCC, Jio Platforms, OPPO) observe that lower-order modulation with higher coding rate can outperform higher-order modulation at the same SE in AWGN-like channels with balanced spatial layers.

    • E-2: 1 source (Fraunhofer IIS/HHI) observes that the existing NR modulation/code-rate choices are close to optimum in AWGN

  • Observation F: (Qm,R) preference under different receiver type

    • F-1: 4 sources (Huawei/HiSilicon, ZTE/Sanechips, Samsung, Ericsson) observe that higher-order modulation with lower coding rate is more favourable with MMSE; Huawei/HiSilicon and ZTE/Sanechips report higher-modulation/lower-rate gains with MMSE but a preference for lower modulation/higher rate with rML; Samsung reports larger higher-modulation gains with MMSE than with K-best, not necessarily a preference reversal. ZTE specifically associates its receiver contrast with high-rank transmission.

    • F-2: 3 sources (Qualcomm, Samsung, Ericsson) observe that lower-order modulation with higher coding rate can provide gains with rML receiver under strong inter-layer interference/high-Doppler or open-loop conditions.

    • F-3: 3 sources (Ericsson, CATT, OPPO) observe that higher-order modulation with lower coding rate can also provide gains with both MMSE and rML in evaluated rank-4 fading-MIMO configurations.

  • Observation G: (Qm,R) preference with respect to MPR (for UL only)

    • G-1: 10 sources (Ericsson, ZTE/Sanechips, Xiaomi, Huawei/HiSilicon, Samsung, vivo, ETRI/SJTU, CATT, Qualcomm, DCM) observe that lower-order modulation with higher coding rate is preferred at selected SE points under UL modulation-dependent power back-off/MPR, especially near modulation transitions.

  • Observation H: (Qm,R) preference dependency on LDPC code

    • H-1: 2 sources (Huawei/HiSilicon, ZTE/Sanechips) report that lower-order modulation with higher coding rate can reduce LDPC decoding complexity at fixed SE, with the evaluated BG3 designs potentially strengthening the associated performance benefit relative to BG1. Higher coding rates reduce decoding-matrix size/active rows, improving decoding throughput or permitting more iterations under the same computational budget.

  • Observation I: (Qm,R) preference dependency on transmit array size

    • I-1: 4 sources (Samsung, Apple, ETRI/SJTU, Ericsson) observe that the higher-modulation advantage is smaller in several evaluated 32Tx4Rx configurations than in 4Tx4Rx configurations.

  • Observation J: On only selected SE regions need alternative pairs

    • J-1: 7 sources (Samsung, Ericsson, Xiaomi, ZTE/Sanechips, Apple, ETRI/SJTU, MTK) observe that modulation-transition regions are especially relevant for alternative modulation/code-rate pairs.
