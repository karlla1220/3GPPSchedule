Observation:

For operation in the same cell or carrier of “UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation (including potential modifications introduced in the Rel-20 GNSS resilience work item)” and “UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation”, the following is observed [from RAN1 perspective]:

  1. For operation in the same cell:

    1. According to current RAN2 specifications (copied below), barring mechanisms prevent both types of UEs from operating in the same NTN cell.

      • Based on the current specification, “UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation (including potential modifications introduced in the Rel-20 GNSS resilience work item)” perform UL pre-compensation in NTN cell only.

      • Based on current specification, an NTN cell can only be deployed in RAN4 defined NTN band.

cellBarredNTN

Value barred means that the cell is barred for connectivity to NTN, as defined in TS 38.304 [20]. Value notBarred means that the cell is allowed for connectivity to NTN. If not present, the UE considers the cell is not allowed for connectivity to NTN, as defined in TS 38.304 [20]. This field is only applicable to NTN-capable UEs. In an NTN cell, the network always includes cellBarredNTN and sets the cellBarred in MIB to barred.

  1. If the real ephemeris information is broadcast:

    • In the uplink, for PRACH, there will be a timing and frequency difference between both types of UEs due to different pre-compensation behavior, requiring the network to handle multiple timing / frequency hypotheses and scheduling with guard bands / periods between both types of UEs.

      • The timing and frequency offset of UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation is common to all such UEs and known to the network.

      • A common TA can be indicated to “UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation (including potential modifications introduced in the Rel-20 GNSS resilience work item)”, to align the transmissions in time between both types of UEs (up to an offset equal to an integer number of slots)

      • After PRACH, TAC can be used to align the transmissions of both types of UEs (up to an offset equal to an integer number of slots)

      • For channels other than PRACH, if the UE does not support FAC (being defined in Rel-20 GNSS resilient work item), there will be a frequency misalignment between both types of UEs, requiring the network to handle multiple frequency hypotheses and scheduling with guard bands between UEs of both types.

    • In the downlink of a cell that performs DL precompensation, the observed Doppler and timing drift at a Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN UE will not match the one expected by the UE after receiving the ephemeris.

      • Some companies mentioned that this mismatch would lead to performance degradation in the downlink reception.

      • Some companies mentioned that, if the UE performs frequent downlink re-synchronization (e.g. by using SSB/TRS every 20ms), the performance degradation is reduced.

  2. If a “synthetic” ephemeris is broadcast:

    • The timing and frequency errors between both types of UEs can be reduced up to some point. For the UEs that process this ephemeris (“UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation”), perfect UE alignment at the satellite in both time and frequency is not possible.

    • Some companies mention that a “synthetic” ephemeris may not comprise a valid orbit and, therefore, the effectiveness of this method may depend on the ephemeris propagation algorithm at the UE.

    • NOTE: A “synthetic” ephemeris consists of a position and velocity vector (different from the real one at the satellite) using existing signaling such that the timing and frequency errors are minimized with respect to UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation.

  1. For operation in the same carrier, the network can provide two overlaid cells (either TDM or FDM), one with downlink pre-compensation and uplink post-compensation (for “UEs not performing uplink pre-compensation.”), and one without downlink pre-compensation and uplink post-compensation (for “UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation”). In this case:

    1. In the uplink, there will be timing and frequency difference between both cells due to different pre-compensation behavior, requiring the network to handle different timing / frequency assumptions in each cell, and scheduling with guard bands / periods between both cells.

      • A common TA can be indicated to “UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation (including potential modifications introduced in the Rel-20 GNSS resilience work item)”, to align the transmissions in time between both types of UEs (up to an offset equal to an integer number of slots)

      • The timing and frequency offset of UEs performing Rel-17/18/19 NR-NTN uplink pre-compensation is common to all such UEs and known to the network.

    2. This solution requires the network to transmit separate broadcast common channels for each of the cells. Some companies mentioned that this increases the signaling overhead by 2x with respect to a single cell solution. Other companies mentioned that, at least in some cases (e.g. 20MHz channel bandwidth), this additional overhead is negligible.

    3. During initial cell search, it is not possible to control which SSB the UE would synchronize to.
