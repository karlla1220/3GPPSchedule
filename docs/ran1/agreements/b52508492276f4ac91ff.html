Observation

The following observations on the CSI codebook for CJT have been made, until RAN1#127:

  • Benefit of CJT and high-resolution CSI for CJT:

    • 4 sources showed that CJT provides UPT gain over sTRP and that a significant fraction of the ideal CJT gain can be realized with high-resolution CSI codebook over different simulation setups/scenarios, e.g., 33~42% in average UPT gain over the sTRP case [Samsung], 4~10% mean and 4~30% 5%-tile UPT gain over sTRP with the gain increasing with RU [CMCC], 1.7x/2.0x/2.1x average UPT gain and 4.4x/5.7x/6.2x 5%-tile UPT gain for 2/3/4 TRPs over the sTRP [Samsung], 18% mean / 30% 5%-tile UPT gain with CJT CSI feedback over sTRP with Rel-16 eType-II [Huawei], and significant fraction (e.g., 20~40%) of the ideal CJT cell-edge gain with high-resolution CSI codebook and with the gain increasing with RU, where SD compression is a major source of loss at high load/cell edge [Ericsson]

    • 3 sources showed that UE-reported CPI can further assist CJT CSI acquisition, e.g., 10% mean / 69% cell-edge UPT gain for mTRP CJT with UE-reported SD/FD CPI [ZTE], 16% mean / 383% cell-edge UPT gain for mTRP SRS-based CSI acquisition with UE-reported CPI [Huawei], ~79% CSI overhead reduction with <1% throughput loss with SDCP/FDCP-based CJT CSI configuration, and 8~19% average / 39~69% 5%-tile UPT gain with SDCP-assisted SRS reception for mTRP compared with DPS [Ericsson]

  • High-resolution scheme with TRP-specific SD basis selection:

    • 4 sources showed that the high-resolution CJT codebook with TRP-specific SD basis selection provides good UPT vs. CSI feedback overhead trade-off, e.g., eType-II CJT with layer-specific SD basis selection per TRP (L=1, 2 per TRP) and TRP-common FD basis outperforms Rel-18 eType-II CJT at a given overhead with the same best parameter combinations as sTRP (3 TRPs, 64 ports per TRP) [Samsung], and 0.3~5.6% mean / 0.8~12.4% cell-edge UPT difference among parameter combinations (78~262 bits) for 3 co-located TRPs with TRP-specific SD basis (L=4 per TRP), i.e., parameter combinations should be optimized [CATT], and compare the performance of Rel-18 CJT Mode-1 and Mode-2 that are with TRP-specific SD basis [vivo], and layer-specific SD basis selection with L=1 or L=2 per TRP offering a competitive cell-edge performance vs. overhead trade-off compared with layer-common schemes, e.g., Rel-18 eType-II CJT codebook [Ericsson].

    • 1 source showed that the major loss of CJT CSI compared with the ideal CJT comes from SD compression, which motivates careful analysis of the SD basis selection scheme. [Ericsson]

    • 19 sources proposed to support the CJT codebook as an extension of the 6GR (sTRP) codebook structure at least for the high-resolution scheme with TRP-specific SD basis selection [Samsung, CATT, vivo, NICT, NEC, Sony, Lenovo, CMCC, InterDigital, Tejas, Ofinno, NTT DOCOMO, OPPO, Ericsson, Apple, ETRI, Huawei, LGE, Jio]

  • TRP-common FD basis selection:

    • 1 source showed that Rel-18 CJT Mode 1 (TRP-specific FD basis) achieves no UPT gain over Mode 2 (TRP-common FD basis) with inter-TRP delay of U[-65, 65] ns [vivo]

    • 2 sources evaluated TRP-common FD basis selection with TRP-specific SD basis selection and showed good performance [Samsung, CATT]

    • 5 sources proposed TRP-common FD basis selection as the baseline (e.g., Rel-18 CJT Mode 2-like) [vivo, NICT, CATT, NEC, Jio], with FFS on delay-domain pre-alignment or TRP-specific FD basis [NICT]

  • Dynamic TRP selection:

    • 2 sources showed that TRP selection (e.g., based on RSRP gap of 20 dB) reduces CSI overhead to ~64% of the codebook without TRP selection with minor/no UPT loss [vivo, Samsung]; 3 sources proposed dynamic TRP selection (NW-indicated or UE-reported) [vivo, NICT, Samsung]

  • Low-resolution scheme for CJT:

    • 1 source showed that a low-resolution CJT codebook with ~34 bits PMI slightly outperforms Rel-18 eType-II CJT PC1 (114~1293 bits) and outperforms all PCs in LOS in the WSMP scenario [NTT DOCOMO]

    • 1 source showed that for M-TRP HST-SFN with limited number of ports per TRP, joint CSI achieves ~40% throughput gain over Rel-17 SFN/sTRP DPS and low-resolution precoding may be sufficient [FHR]

    • 5 sources proposed to study the low-resolution CJT codebook (e.g., TRP-specific SD basis with inter-TRP co-phasing) [Samsung, CATT, NICT, NTT DOCOMO, Qualcomm]

  • 9 sources proposed a common/unified codebook structure for sTRP and CJT (e.g., reducing to sTRP codebook for N_TRP=1) with the same parameter design to the extent possible [Samsung, NICT, Sony, NEC, Lenovo, InterDigital, Tejas, OPPO, LGE]

  • Note: 2~4 TRPs, intra-/inter-site deployments with ideal backhaul and inter-TRP synchronization, 8~64 ports per TRP, SU/MU-MIMO and FTP traffic are assumed.

    • When inter-TRP synchronization is non-ideal, 1 source showed significant CJT performance degradation due to residual inter-TRP synchronization/calibration errors, e.g., ~10.3–13.4% mean and ~21.6–28.7% cell-edge UPT loss with 46 ns DO and 12 Hz FO under RAN4-defined highest-accuracy requirements [Qualcomm].

Observation

The following observations on CJT calibration report have been made until RAN1#127:

  • Without calibration, inter-TRP delay, frequency and phase offsets cause considerable UPT loss for CJT, e.g.,

    • delay offset: uncompensated delay offset among 6 TRPs reduces the median/5%-tile SE by ~49%/~57% [Sony], and CJT without CJTC report achieves only ~1/3 of the average UPT with delay offset compensation in inter-site CJT with ISI modelled [Samsung]

    • phase offset: non-calibrated CJT achieves 92.4%~93.4% of the average UPT with ideal calibration [CATT]

  • With small feedback overhead, the standalone NR CJTC reports (i.e., delay offset (DO), frequency offset (FO) and phase offset (PO) reports) recover most of the loss and provide large UPT gain for CJT in various scenarios, as shown by 7 sources [Samsung, ZTE, Sony, vivo, Ericsson, CATT, Huawei], while 1 source showed that CJTC may not fully recover the loss when FO varies dynamically due to Doppler [Qualcomm]:

    • CJTC-DO report:

      • 1 source [Samsung] showed 2.5~3x average UPT compared with CJT without CJTC report (TRP selection: ~2.3x; delay offset compensation: ~2.9~3.0x) in inter-site CJT with ISI modelled

      • 1 source [Sony] showed that delay offset compensation restores the median SE to within ~1% of the ideal case and increases the 5%-tile SE by 2.1x (~90% of the ideal case) for 6 TRPs

      • 1 source [Sony] showed ~80%/~90% average UPT gain with CJTC report for 4/6 TRPs

      • 1 source [ZTE] showed 6.6% average and 27.2% cell-edge UPT gain

      • 1 source [vivo] showed that even with the worst-case residual delay offset of 130 ns after calibration, UE-side compensation provides 2~3% average UPT gain

    • CJTC-FO report: 2 sources showed 7.8% average and 30.3% cell-edge UPT gain [ZTE], and ~50%/~40% UPT gain with ideal FO calibration / FO estimation error (5 levels) at 3 dB SNR [Huawei]. 1 source [Ericsson] showed that finer FO reporting resolution (5/8 bits) improves the throughput by 35.5%/65.1% compared with 4-bit FO report. 1 source [Qualcomm] showed residual frequency offset of 33 Hz / 12 Hz incurs ~26.7% / ~7.6% average and ~35.7% / ~11.9% cell-edge UPT loss with Full buffer traffic modelling. 

    • CJTC-DO and CJTC-FO reports (reported together in one instance or separately): 2 sources showed combined benefits, i.e., 17.8% average and 56.4% cell-edge UPT gain [ZTE], and 51.5% throughput gain in dense UMa mTRP scenario [Ericsson]. 1 source showed residual DO of 46 ns and FO of 12 Hz incur 10.3~13.4% (cell center) / 21.6~28.7% (cell edge) UPT loss for both low- and high-resolution CJT schemes [Qualcomm].

    • CJTC-PO report: 3 sources showed UPT gain with PO report, e.g., 6.7%~9.9% average / 7.1%~8.8% cell-edge UPT gain with subband PO report for 2/4 GHz, while the wideband phase offset report cannot solve the DL/UL time misalignment [CATT], ~8% average UPT gain for TDD reciprocity-based CJT [Samsung], and 50.8% throughput gain in multilink reciprocity-based CJT [Ericsson]

  • Regarding the reporting resolution and dynamic range, 3 sources showed that the NR (Rel-19) or comparable resolution/dynamic range achieves near-ideal performance:

    • 1 source [Samsung] showed that the CJTC-DO report with 5-bit quantization achieves ~3x average UPT compared with ~3x with ideal (unquantized) delay offset

    • 1 source [Qualcomm] showed that the Rel-19 NR dynamic range and resolution of DO/FO reports are sufficient, and the CJT performance is mainly limited by the measurement accuracy

    • 1 source [CATT] showed that the subband PO report with 4~5-bit quantization (8-PRB subband) achieves 99.6%~99.8% of the average UPT with ideal calibration, compared with 93.4%~93.8% with wideband PO report

    • 1 source [Samsung] showed that the CJTC-PO report with 4-bit quantization achieves almost the ideal performance, and even 1-bit quantization provides ~5.5% average UPT gain

  • Regarding the measurement error modelling, 1 source [Qualcomm] showed that with static error modelling, DO/FO measurement error incurs ~0.1~0.4% / ~1.0~22% average UPT loss, while with dynamic (time-varying) error modelling, it incurs ~0.5~7.8% / ~5.4~27% average UPT loss w.r.t. ideal calibration, and the FO drift between calibration updates incurs additional ~4% UPT loss

  • Regarding the linkage between the CJTC report and the CJT-codebook-based CSI report, 1 source [vivo] showed that the CJT CSI report is robust to the residual delay offset (e.g., up to 130 ns) after the standalone delay offset report

  • Note: 2~6 TRPs, intra-/inter-site deployments, 16~64 ports per TRP, 2/3.5/4 GHz, and FTP traffic are assumed
