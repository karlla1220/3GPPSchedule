Observation

  • Seven companies’ simulation results demonstrated that aperiodic RS in addition to periodic RS-based finer time and frequency tracking can provide performance benefits in certain conditions [Nokia, OPPO, ZTE, Samsung, Qualcomm, Apple, MediaTek], e.g.,

    • Nokia indicated that additional aperiodic tracking provides benefits when:

      • 20ms or 40ms periodic RS is used with 1024QAM, where periodic RS only tracking requires approximately 4~5 dB more SNR than ideal tracking; and

      • 80ms periodic RS is used, where performance degradation increases with modulation order, including clearly higher required SNR for QPSK and 16QAM and a BLER floor or inability to reach the target within the evaluated SNR range from 64QAM upward.

    • MediaTek indicated that complementary use of PDSCH DMRS can provide benefits when the periodic RS bandwidth is smaller than the PDSCH bandwidth; for example, 52RB/20ms periodic RS + PDSCH DMRS achieved performance comparable to 273RB/20ms periodic RS without complementary PDSCH DMRS processing.

    • Samsung observed that Periodic RS with 80ms periodicity + scheduling PDCCH DMRS based tracking can provide lower BLER and higher throughput than periodic RS only based tracking, with about 0.4 dB and 0.7 dB SNR gain at 10% BLER for 16QAM with a half-band and a full-band CORESET, respectively.

    • Qualcomm observed approximately 0.7/0.8 dB gains for MCS 9 at 40/80 ms, respectively, and approximately 0.8 dB gain for MCS 16 at 40ms, at 10% PDSCH BLER. For MCS 16 at 80 ms, periodic-TRS-only tracking does not reach 10% BLER in the evaluated range, while periodic TRS + AP-TRS does, indicating a gain of more than 6dB.

    • ZTE observed that without aperiodic RS, periodic RS with 160ms periodicity only based tracking, 256QAM cannot achieve 10% BLER in the SNR rang 2~16dB.

    • Apple observed that 80ms periodic RS alone cannot achieve 10% PDSCH BLER for 256QAM, while periodic RS + aperiodic RS can achieve the 10% BLER target.

Observation

  • Four companies showed that increasing the periodic RS periodicity can degrade link performance when periodic RS is the only tracking RS, with the severity depending on modulation order and the evaluated SNR range [OPPO, Nokia, ZTE, Qualcomm], e.g.

    • ZTE’s results indicated that compared with 20ms periodicity, 40ms periodicity has ~0.5-0.7dB loss, 80ms periodicity has ~4dB loss, while for 160ms, its BLER has already exceeded 0.1 for 256QAM.

    • OPPO’s results indicated that

      • increasing the periodic RS periodicity from 20ms to 40ms results in >4dB BLER degradation for periodic RS only tracking for QPSK, 16QAM and 64QAM.

      • with periodic RS periodicity of 40ms or longer, QPSK, 16QAM, and 64QAM achieved 10% BLER larger than 10dB, 20dB and 30dB respectively.

    • Nokia’s results indicated that,

      • with periodic RS as the only tracking RS, 20ms and 40ms periodicities give similar PDSCH performance up to 256QAM (within about 0.5 dB at 10% BLER).

      • with 80ms periodicity, QPSK needs about 2dB higher SNR than 20ms and 40ms periodicity to reach 10% PDSCH BLER (about 9.5 dB vs. 7.5 dB), and 16QAM is clearly degraded compared with 20ms and 40ms periodicity. From 64QAM upward, 10% PDSCH BLER is not achieved within the evaluated SNR range.

    • Qualcomm’s results indicated that increasing the periodic RS periodicity from 20ms to 40/80/160ms results in approximately 1/2.2/5 dB additional loss for 16QAM (MCS 9) and approximately 1.2/4/6.7 dB additional loss for 64QAM (MCS 16) at 10% PDSCH BLER.

Observation

On the comparison between option 1 and option 2 for aperiodic RS for finer time and frequency tracking,

  • Four companies’ simulation results demonstrated benefits of PDSCH DMRS-based finer time and frequency tracking in complementing periodic RS-based tracking [Ericsson, ETRI, MediaTek, ZTE] and two companies [Nokia, ZTE] compared periodic RS + PDSCH DMRS-based tracking with periodic RS + aperiodic dedicated RS-based tracking, under their respective evaluation assumptions and configurations. E.g.,

    • Nokia’s results indicated that, with double-symbol DMRS in the first two slots of the burst and front-loaded DMRS thereafter, PDSCH DMRS-based tracking is close to ideal tracking up to 1024QAM, whereas front-loaded only can exhibit a BLER floor of approximately 5–10% for the evaluated higher-MCS cases. A dedicated aperiodic RS (Option 1) is about 2 dB from ideal tracking for 1024QAM. The evaluated AP-TRS and PDSCH DMRS configurations differ in RS bandwidth, timing and density; in particular, AP-TRS is 52 RB and provided at specific occasions relative to the PDSCH burst (3ms before the first PDSCH slot and 13 ms within the 20ms PDSCH burst), whereas the PDSCH DMRS spans the scheduled PDSCH bandwidth and is available within the PDSCH burst.

    • ZTE’s results indicated that both aperiodic TRS with precoding and PDSCH DMRS with precoding provide performance gains compared with aperiodic TRS without precoding by at least 1dB in BLER. In the evaluated configurations, the 52-RB precoded AP-TRS with precoding provides better raw BLER performance than the 24-RB PDSCH DMRS with precoding, corresponding to approximately 0.5-1dB at 10% BLER and larger at lower BLER, Conversely, PDSCH DMRS with precoding provides higher effective spectral efficiency after accounting for the overhead of aperiodic TRS with precoding, corresponding to approximately 10% and 19% spectral efficiency gains at 14dB SNR for 30 and 60 active UEs, respectively and such gain becomes more pronounced as the number of active UEs in a cell increases.

    • ETRI’s results indicated that complementary PDSCH-DMRS processing reduces the required SNR at 10% PDSCH BLER by approximately 0.3–1.1 dB, depending on periodic-RS periodicity and modulation. A separate decomposition for 256QAM showed that residual frequency-offset correction and phase-aligned averaging provide larger contribution, compared with incorporating accumulated DMRS delay-domain information. A dedicated aperiodic RS was not evaluated.

    • MediaTek’s results indicated that using PDSCH DM-RS in addition to periodic RS with 52 RB bandwidth and 20ms periodicity can achieve comparable tracking performance to a periodic RS with 273 RB bandwidth and 20ms periodicity for PDSCH with 100MHz BW, both can reach 10% PDSCH BLER with required SNR approximately 10 dB. A dedicated aperiodic RS alternative was not evaluated in this comparison.

    • Ericsson’s results indicated that, at 4 GHz and 7 GHz carrier frequencies, DMRS-based tracking provides benefits for finer tracking, including a 0.5dB SNR gain (at 4 GHz) and a 1.6 dB SNR gain (at 7 GHz) in low-SNR regimes. These link level results assume rank and link are enabled. These results demonstrate benefits of DMRS-assisted tracking but do not directly compare periodic RS + DMRS against periodic RS + dedicated aperiodic RS under matched conditions.

    • In the above evaluated periodic RS + dedicated AP-TRS configurations the complementary use of the available PDSCH DMRS for tracking by UE implementation is not accounted for.

    • In the above evaluated periodic RS + PDSCH-DMRS, wideband PRG, single continuous PDSCH allocation, 2 DMRS symbols in a slot were used.

  • One company’s simulation result demonstrated that periodic RS + scheduling PDCCH-DMRS-based finer time and frequency tracking provided comparable or better performance, e.g., 0.5dB SNR gain for 16QAM @BLER 0.1, depending on the evaluated bandwidth and metric, without additional dedicated tracking-RS overhead, compared with periodic RS + dedicated aperiodic-RS-based tracking. In particular, full-band PDCCH DMRS provided BLER similar to precoder-aligned dedicated aperiodic RS, while providing higher throughput at medium and high SNR when the additional overhead of the dedicated RS was included. The evaluated PDCCH-DMRS-based alternative assumes that the PDCCH DMRS uses the PDSCH precoder and that the UE knows the corresponding precoder relationship. [Samsung]

  • Two companies’ simulation results demonstrated benefits of using a dedicated aperiodic RS in addition to periodic RS for finer time and frequency tracking, particularly when periodic-RS periodicity is relatively long or when an additional tracking update is needed close to the scheduled PDSCH [Qualcomm, Apple].

    • Qualcomm’s results indicated that when periodic TRS is configured with relatively long periodicities, such as 40ms or 80ms, complementing it with an AP-TRS before PDSCH can improve BLER performance.

    • Apple indicated that, with periodic RS of 80ms, the configuration using the PDSCH DMRS available with the scheduled PDSCH has a BLER of about 4.5% at 32 dB, whereas adding a one-slot dedicated aperiodic RS before each served PDSCH burst achieves BLER below 1% from 26dB.

    • Apple’s evaluation additionally showed the benefit of the dedicated aperiodic RS when PDSCH DMRS was already used for tracking by UE implementation in all evaluated schemes.

Observation

For joint DL/UL CSI acquisition use case 1A,

  • Six companies’ simulation results demonstrated that covariance channel matrix/spectrum assisted SRS channel estimation provided performance gain or loss compared with Baseline#1(DL CSI acquisition based on SRS only) [ZTE, Huawei, Qualcomm, Apple, Lenovo, OPPO], e.g.,

    • Lenovo observed NMSE gain of 0.7 to 1.9 dB for 4RX aNB ports and 1.5 to 4 dB for 16RX aNB ports with SD covariance matrix reporting

    • Qualcomm observed that

      • SD covariance matrix reporting without quantization assisted SRS channel estimation provides +76% and 4% gain in terms of 5% and 50% UPT for 32 TxRUs, and +53% and +5% gain in terms of 5% and 50% UPT for 128 TxRUs.

      • Joint SD/FD covariance reporting without quantization assisted SRS channel estimation provides +85% gain and 2% loss in terms of 5% and 50% UPT for 32 TxRUs, and +56% and 4% gain in terms of 5% and 50% UPT for 128 TxRUs.

    • ZTE observed that SD covariance matrix reporting provides UPT gain of 10% and 69% for cell average and cell edge, respectively for multi-TRP transmission, compared with Baseline#1(DL CSI acquisition based on SRS only).

    • Huawei observed that compared with single-TRP SRS based CSI acquisition with SRS based CPI, UE reported CPI provides gain of 9.5% and 152% for cell average and cell edge, respectively. For multi-TRP, UE reported CPI provides gain of 13.7% and 258% for cell average and cell edge, respectively.

    • Apple observed that the covariance reported by the UE from the DL, without quantization and DL measurement error, provided 0.91 dB NMSE gain over Baseline#1 where the aNB uses the covariance matrix estimated from the received SRS, and 5.11 dB over raw LS channel estimation, with the aNB Tx/Rx calibration error modeled.

    • OPPO observed that, compared with unfiltered SRS based CSI acquisition for single-TRP transmission without CPI, UE reported spectrum-based CPI provides UPT gain by +199.80%, and SRS channel estimation NMSE can decrease by about 99% for cell-edge UEs.

  • One company’s simulation result demonstrated that PMI (eType PMI with PC6) reporting assisted SRS channel estimation provided positive performance gain, compared with Baseline#1(DL CSI acquisition based on SRS only), [Qualcomm] e.g.,

    • 92% and 4% gain in terms of 5% and 50% UPT for 32 TxRUs, and +70% and 2% gain in terms of 5% and 50% UPT for 128 TxRUs

  • One company’s simulation result demonstrated that both joint SD/FD and SD only covariance reporting without quantization assisted SRS channel estimation underperform Baseline #2 (DL CSI acquisition based on CSI-RS only) in low-to-medium DL SNR range, [Qualcomm], e.g.

    • In the DL SNR range [-10, 5] dB, NR Type I CSI based DL CSI acquisition provides 9~11% TPUT gain over DL CSI acquisition based on joint SD/FD or SD only covariance reporting assisted SRS channel estimation.

  • One company’s simulation result demonstrated that PMI (eType PMI with PC6) reporting assisted SRS channel estimation provided better performance gain compared with covariance channel matrix/spectrum assisted SRS channel estimation [Qualcomm], compared with Baseline #1 (DL CSI acquisition based on SRS only), e.g.,

    • PMI (eType PMI with PC6eT2) CSI reporting assisted SRS channel estimation provides +7% and 14% gain in terms of 5% UPT for 32 TxRUs and 128 TxRUs, respectively.

  • Two companies’ simulation result demonstrated that covariance channel matrix/spectrum assisted SRS channel estimation provides better performance gain compared with PMI reporting assisted SRS channel estimation [Huawei, ZTE], e.g.,

    • ZTE observed that UE-reported covariance matrix outperforms both Type I PMI-based covariance matrix and eType II PMI-based covariance matrix over the entire SNR range, e.g.,

      • compared with the Type I PMI-based covariance matrix, the UE-reported covariance matrix provides performance gain of approximately 200% and 100% in SE at 6 dB SNR and 8 dB SNR, respectively, and compared with the eType II PC4 PMI-based covariance matrix, the UE-reported covariance matrix provides performance gain of approximately 200% and 80% in SE at 6 dB SNR and 8 dB SNR, respectively.

      • for the PMI-based covariance matrix under different RI values, the performance degradation becomes more significant as the reported RI decreases.

        • Compared with the etype2 PMI-based covariance matrix under rank3, the etype2 PMI-based covariance matrix under rank4 provides a performance gain of up to 10%. Compared with the etype2 PMI-based covariance matrix under rank2, the etype2 PMI-based covariance matrix under rank3 provides a performance gain of up to 46%; and compared with the etype2 PMI-based covariance matrix under rank1, the etype2 PMI-based covariance matrix under rank2 provides a performance gain of up to 180%.

    • Huawei observed performance gain of UE reported CPI compared to CPI derived from eTypeII PMI

      • For the same CPI reporting overhead (around 300 bits one time) with 256 TX ports, CPI reporting with non-orthogonal DFT basis and associated power achieves performance gain of 34% and 8.8% for cell edge and cell average respectively, compared to CPI based on eTypeII PC3 PMI.

      • For the CPI reporting overhead (around 300 bits one time) with 256 TX ports, CPI reporting with non-orthogonal DFT basis and associated power achieves performance gain of 26.8% and 6.8% for cell edge and cell average respectively, compared to CPI based on eTypeII PC6 PMI, with reporting overhead of around 680 bits one time.

      • For 32 TX ports, CPI reporting with non-orthogonal DFT basis and associated power (with overhead of 100 bits one time) achieves performance gain of 9% and 2.9% for cell edge and cell average respectively, compared to CPI based on eTypeII PMI PC3 with reporting overhead 300 bits one time.

      • For 32 TX ports, CPI reporting with non-orthogonal DFT basis and associated power (with overhead of 100 bits one time) achieves performance gain of 5% and 1% for cell edge and cell average respectively, compared to CPI based on eTypeII PMI PC6 with reporting overhead of around 680 bits one time.

    • Note: The assumptions on the DL measurement error and the quantization of the reported assistance information differ among the evaluations, and there is no agreed error model for the DL measurement of the assistance information.

    • for wideband PDSCH precoding, the throughput gain can reach 10% for single port precoded TRS to 15% for multi-port precoded TRS;

    • for subband PDSCH precoding, the throughput gain can reach 19% for single port precoded TRS to 22% for multi-port precoded TRS;

  • One company [Ericsson] observed that

    • DMRS-based and non-precoded TRS-based tracking achieve similar user throughput across the full SNR range in both full-buffer and C-DRX operating modes at lower carrier frequencies.

    • DMRS-based tracking outperforms non-precoded TRS-based tracking in low-SNR regimes at high carrier frequency, wherein the following gains are observed at 7 GHz carrier frequency at low-SNR regimes:

      • In C-DRX operation mode: 1.6 dB SNR gain

      • In full-buffer operation mode: 1.8 dB SNR gain

    • At 10% BLER, using 0.1× the applicable PDP as a UE’s estimated PDP, results in approximately 0.7–1 dB degradation for MCS 9, 15, and 20, and approximately 1.8 dB degradation for MCS 25, compared with using the applicable PDP. Observation

For RS for finer time and frequency tracking for larger channel bandwidth

  • One company’s simulation result demonstrated that separate finer time and frequency tracking for different subband of the channel bandwidth can provided performance benefit with the assumption that separate RF chain with the following results. [OPPO]

    • A single whole-band CFO estimate based on full-BWP TRS leaves a structural band-averaged residual CFO that does not improve with SNR (≈112 Hz in the evaluated case, consistent with ~½ of the inter-chain oscillator CFO difference).

    • RF-aligned per-sub-band/per-RF-chain processing of the RS used for finer time and frequency tracking with independent CFO tracking removes the structural residual floor and reduces the band-averaged residual CFO to a few Hz at moderate-to-high SNR (≈3–4 Hz at 20 dB in the evaluated case), yielding about 30× gain over single whole-band CFO tracking.

    • A narrower per-chain TRS span (e.g., 52 RB) remains effective for dual-RF residual CFO tracking compared with a near-full-chain span (e.g., 555 RB), with only a modest residual increase due to higher estimation noise, while both configurations substantially outperform full-BWP single-CFO tracking.

  • One company’s simulation result demonstrated that with one UE oscillator reference the frequency tracking needs no bandwidth-specific design (option 0): the CFO RMSE at 400 MHz is practically the same for a 52RB and a 1092RB tracking RS (140 and 141Hz). [Apple]

  • One company’s simulation result demonstrated that option 0 is sufficient for a UE with a common LO while option 1 is beneficial for a UE with separate LO with the following results. [Nokia]

    • When the UE receives a channel bandwidth larger than 200 MHz with a common LO, finer time and frequency tracking with joint estimation over the subbands performs identically to a contiguous carrier

    • When the subbands are received with independent LOs, per subband processing of the RS used for finer time and frequency tracking per subband keeps the PDSCH throughput within 2% of a contiguous carrier with 40ms periodic RS and aperiodic RS, whereas a tracking RS in only one subband results in 100% BLER at MCS 20 for a 0.1 ppm frequency difference between the subbands

  • One company’s simulation result demonstrated that separate finer time and frequency tracking for different subband of the channel bandwidth can reduce the CFO RMSE by about 30% with the assumption that single RF chain, e.g., the tracking RS is transmitted in centre 52PRBs in each 200MHz. [Xiaomi]
